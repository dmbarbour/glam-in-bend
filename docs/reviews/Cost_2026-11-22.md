# Instructions against what they buy: first findings

`ladder-measure.roi` in `docs/plans/HelloLadder_2026-11-09.md`, at the
maintainer's request (2026-11-22): "I am concerned at the number of
instructions growing out of control. In the spike, IIRC we were managing
a few hundred instructions per interaction. ... we should soon review
whether we're getting an adequate ROI and what we can trim." First
findings, measured on one workload; each a finding with a proposed
verdict, for the maintainer to direct before anything is trimmed.

**How measured.** The cursor workload `seq` at 1,600 elements
(`scripts/cursors.py`), main at 17e943f: 189K instructions an element in
the default build, for about 25 interactions, 6 cursor interactions, 28
nodes spliced, 2 serves and 3 splices an element. The assembler's C
compiled with frame pointers (`clang -O2 -g -fno-omit-frame-pointer`),
`perf record -g -e instructions:u -c 100000`, about 3,100 samples; each
sample charged to the first named Bend function on its stack, so that
the runtime's work lands on the function it runs under; hot call sites
resolved to lines of the C, whose variables keep their Bend names. The
book's loop is `book_get` and `book_set` (`glam/core.bend`), a walk by
index down a `List<&2, Ent>` the core passes as a copyable parameter. Notes
and the profile's tables: `results/measure/roi/seq1600_profile.md`, out
of git.

## Findings

**C1. Where an element's instructions go, by layer.** The core (frames,
chasing cells, needs, endings) 31.6%; the front end (parse 7.1%, lower
9.6%, layout 2.9%, desugar 2.4%, lex 1.3%) 23.3%; demand's walk 11.0%;
the splice 9.0%; the serve's materialize 8.6%; freeze 7.1%; positioned
demand 1.7%; the kernel's primitives 0.9%. The kernel's own work, the
interactions, is a small share: on its standard workloads it costs 590
to 940 instructions an interaction (store run 337), and `seq` makes 25
interactions an element, near 20K of its 189K. Verdict: the machinery
around the kernel, not the kernel, is the cost.

**C2. Half of all instructions are Bend's reference counting.** The
runtime's `term_drop` (31%), `span_fade` (15%) and `rfc_wrap` (5%) are
52% of the samples. `span_fade` is the fallback of a match on a
constructor still shared: every field's count bumped, the container
dropped (`ctr_take`); `term_drop` the release of what a branch leaves
unused, a whole list or record when it is the last reference. So the
cost follows from how values are shared and dropped, more than from
what is computed. Verdict: the largest lever; the items below are its
sites.

**C3. The core's book of cells is a list walked for every lookup, its
cells shared.** One loop, a walk of `b` for a key (`spin_1922` in the C,
four call sites), is about 12% of all instructions, nearly all in
`span_fade` and `term_drop`: each step of a lookup matches a shared
cons cell. The book holds an entry a cell (`Ent`), looked up by number
(`chase`, `book_set`). Verdict: an indexed book, an array or map by cell
number, unshared; lookups then constant and with no per-entry count
traffic.

**C4. A splice walks its template's node list, the template shared.**
`sp.nodes` (`glam/splice_copy.bend`; `spin_2077`) is about 6.4%, again
a match on a shared cell a node. Verdict: templates as arrays or a flat
format (`cells-freeze.format`, the prefix bytecode), read without
taking a list apart; or the splice reading a template it owns.

**C5. Whole values dropped where a branch does not use them.** The
core's ending drops its lower segments, its book and its sources
(`core_ended`, `term_sink` of `low`, `b`, `ss`, 2.5%); materialize
drops template pieces it took (`materialize_k4395`, 4.2% over two
sites). Verdict: thread such values only where they are used, or keep
them in state that is not rebuilt each step; an audit of the core's
records (`Mv`, `Cx`, `Lo`) for fields carried through steps that do not
need them.

**C6. The front end is a fifth of the cost.** 23% here, 70K
instructions an element, flat in the list's length; quadratic in a
program's definitions and in names in one scope
(`ladder-measure.names`, planned). Verdict: as planned, names compared
without copying and definitions keyed once.

**C7. Demand's walk is 11%, after the climb.** Positioned demand's
walks were the growth (`cells-freeze.sharing.cursors.growth`); what is
left is the host's own demand and the walks that do reduce. Verdict:
measure again after C3 to C5, when it is a larger share of less.

## What a target could be

The kernel's 600 to 900 instructions an interaction is the floor this
code can reach on its own work. Removing C3 and C4's count traffic
would take about 18% of `seq`; C6 most of 23% on programs of many
definitions. A target per interaction needs the maintainer's view of
what the layers above the kernel should cost; the agent proposes
measuring each of C3 to C6 as built, n, 2n and 4n, against
`scripts/cursors.py` and the ladder, and setting the target after C3.

## Order proposed

C3 (the book), C6 (names, planned), C4 (with `cells-freeze.format` or
alone), C5 (an audit), then C7 measured again.
