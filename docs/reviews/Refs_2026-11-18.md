# References — 2026-11-18

A review of how references are encoded and how far the laws state the
encoding, for the Cells plan's `cells-freeze.nested.host.repr`
([`../plans/Cells_2026-11-09.md`](../plans/Cells_2026-11-09.md)), at the
maintainer's request (2026-11-18): "TBH, I wonder if we'd be better off
with bits `00` meaning static, `01`-`11` corresponding to ports on a
node. This could simplify our bit-banging logic a little bit. Our
expression of laws should ideally abstract the representation details a
bit more, moving those to proof lemmas where feasible."

Two reviewers: a Fable sub-agent, whose review is kept as written in
[`RefsFable_2026-11-18.md`](RefsFable_2026-11-18.md) (findings
`RefsFable F1` to `F11`), and the agent, here. The agent read the second
review first, checked it against the source, and adds findings `R1` to
`R5`. This review proposes; the maintainer decides.

Two questions, as the second review names them: A, the encoding (statics
at port bits `00`, a node's ports at `01` to `11`); B, laws that name
abstractions of the encoding, its details in definitions and proof
lemmas.

## The agent's findings

**R1. Under the proposal, a base passed for a reference is a
well-formed static.** Today a node's base, its header's address and its
principal port's reference are one number, so passing one for another
cannot go wrong (`RefsFable F4`). Under the proposal the base keeps bits
`00`, the static tag: a site that misses the `+ 1` hands its peer a
static, not a bad reference. While the eraser is the only static, the
rewrite path counts any other static as a fault (`knet.bend:1130-1131`)
and the audit demands the eraser of a static (`wa.lk`, 1555). Once
immediates exist, a base reads as a valid integer and nothing can tell.
So if A is taken, it comes before immediates land, with the audit
asserting that the eraser is the only static until the change is done.

**R2. B in two slices.** B1, the reference encoding: a port's link word
as a `Maybe`, none for a static; `port.ok` guarded by it; the frame
laws' premises through it; the free list's node address named
(`P.node_at`, `RefsFable §5`). That slice is what A would change and
what `RefsFable F7` and `F8` need. B2, the ring's node layout: a
cursor's words 1, 2 and 3 named instead of `m + 1`, `m + 2`, `m + 3`.
That is glam's own layout, which A leaves alone; it is readability, and
can wait or not happen.

**R3. B1 weakens some conclusions, so it needs the maintainer's yes
as a weakening.** Guarding `port.ok` makes `ring.ok`, `node.ok`,
`enter.ok` and `pend.ok` hold of more heaps. Of the 15 laws over them,
six take them only as premises (`join_keeps`, `join1_keeps`,
`cursor_head`, `cursor_pos`, `crossings_keeps`, `leave_keeps`) and get
stronger. Nine also conclude them (`insert_ok`, `cursor_ok`,
`cursor_pend`, `join1_pend`, `cross_pend`, `crossings_pend`,
`join_exact`, `join1_exact`, `leave_exact`), and for a static face they
no longer claim that `face + 1` is off the members' words. That word is
no link word and nothing writes it, so nothing true is lost. But by the
rule a law's conclusion changes, so the approval should name it.

**R4. Witness heaps could be built, not spelled.** Every glam witness is
a literal heap: `Array.set` of encoded words, the eraser's `4294967295`,
references such as `13, 41` (`glam/MUTANTS.txt`). Under A each is
re-derived by hand; the second review counts them as the larger part of
A's cost. Witnesses that build their heaps with the code's own names (a
node placed, two ports joined by `P.link`) would change with the
encoding, not by hand, and would read as the net they stand for. To try
on one witness first: the witness file has the law file's imports, so
the builders must live where `LAWS.bend` can reach them.

**R5. Where B's model lives.** As the second review proposes: a
heap-level model in `spike/` that both `LAWS.bend` files import
(`spike/LAWS.bend` cannot import glam's `RG`), with `RG`'s names kept as
one-line aliases so that `glam/PROOF.bend` does not move. The code's
`link.to` could instead match on a `Maybe` link word, so that the law
names a function the program calls (`docs/Proving.md`, State a law
over what the program builds). But it sits on the rewrite's hot path,
and a `Maybe` there is untested; a model is the safer first step.

## Recommendation

1. **B1 now**, on a side branch:
   - a heap-level `lw(r) : Maybe<U32>` and `off.port`;
   - `port.ok` guarded by them;
   - `join_keeps`, `join1_keeps`, `leave_keeps` and `link_keeps`
     stating their link-word premises through `off.port` (which repairs
     `join1_keeps`'s premises, as the plan names);
   - the free list's `((fh - 1) << 2n)` named.

   The proofs mostly wrap what exists: `lx_keeps.s` and `join1_keeps.ms`
   are `off.port` unfolded already. One merge, with every law it changes
   in a `Law:` trailer, the weakened conclusions named (R3).
2. **A decided with `netdata-refs`.** The one gain beyond reading is
   integers that add under their tag (`RefsFable §2`); whether to tag
   integers `00` decides A. If A is taken:
   - before immediates land (R1);
   - ports kept 0-based in templates and tables (`RefsFable` P-a);
   - the eraser not 0, so that a zero-filled word is not read as erased
     (`RefsFable F3`);
   - the old store kept on its own encoding as the differential test's
     oracle;
   - measured four ways, no case beyond the forced-inline build's noise
     (`RefsFable F6`).
3. **B2 and witness builders (R4)** when convenient: neither is needed by
   A or by the serve's proofs.

## Questions for the maintainer

1. B1 as above, with the nine conclusions over `ring.ok` and `pend.ok`
   that claim less for a static face (R3): approve the slice?
2. `netdata-refs`: should integers take a `00` tag, so that tagged
   integers add and compare without untagging? That decides A.
3. If A: which value is the eraser, and is nil still the eraser's
   value, or 0, "none" as the free list's 0 is (`RefsFable F2`, `F3`)?
4. If A: ports kept 0-based in templates and tables, or renumbered 1 to
   3 (`RefsFable` P-a, P-b; about 30 more sites for the latter)?
5. Witness heaps built from the code's names (R4): worth a trial?

## The maintainer's answers (2026-11-18)

1. B1: "Sure."
2. On the integers' tag: "IIUC, it would be suffix `(0)(00)` for
   integers in netadata refs if we do this, i.e. the tag for integers is
   `0` not `00`. We might be able to take advantage of that." Under the
   proposal an integer is a word whose low three bits are `000`, `n`
   stored as `n << 3`: two of them add and subtract to the stored sum,
   and compare as the integers do, with no untagging.
3. On the zero word: "A zero-filled word would read as a zero integer,
   IIUC. Though, if we want to eliminate zero-filled words, we could use
   a one's complement encoding for small integers and eliminate the
   all-zeroes encoding of zero. This would pun a 0 word with undefined.
   Not sure of the value of trying to avoid assigning meaning to a zero,
   though." Open. What a meaning for zero costs: today a zero link word
   is node 0's port 0, inside the net's header, which the audit rejects
   (`wa.at`, `spike/knet.bend:1557`); as the integer 0 it would pass for
   a value, and a port never linked would go unnoticed. What avoiding it
   costs: zero's encoding no longer all zeroes, so tagged arithmetic
   pays a correction where a result is zero.
4. On renumbering ports: "How we name ports is independent. We could call
   them principal, aux1, aux2. We could give them many other names,
   whatever is convenient for us." The question dissolves: the code names
   ports as it likes, and the encoding gives each name its bits.
5. Witness builders: "Sure."

And on question 3, after B1 (2026-11-18): "Eventually, we'll want to
resolve more issues with laws than with dynamic audits. I.e. laws to let
us be confident that every meaningful word of every node kind is
properly assigned would be worth more than auditing and hoping an
accidentally not-assigned link word has a zero value." So a zero word
need not be kept free of meaning for an audit's sake: laws that every
meaningful word is written are the check
(`cells-freeze.nested.host.assigned`).

## Corrections (2026-11-18)

Rechecked while building B1:

- **`RefsFable F7` is wrong as stated.** `RG.port.ok` holds of every
  static, not of the eraser by its value: `bases` makes every member a
  multiple of four (`P.base(m) == m`, `glam/ring_model.bend:255`), and
  `apartw(nw(a), d)` in `layout` keeps a node's four words in distinct
  slots, so the heap's depth is at least 2 and a slot keeps a word's low
  two bits. A member's word 2 ends in `10`, its word 3 in `11`; a
  static's `r + 1` ends in `00`, so it is in neither. Every predicate
  using `port.ok` (`ring.ok`, `node.ok`, `enter.ok`, `pend.ok`) has
  `bases` or the face's own base test beside it. The model relies on
  the encoding, a node's words aligned, not on the eraser's value; its
  comment said otherwise and is corrected. Guarding `port.ok` for
  statics would change no law's meaning.
- **R3 is withdrawn.** It followed from F7: with `port.ok` unchanged in
  meaning, no conclusion over `ring.ok` or `pend.ok` claims less. B1
  is the frame laws alone (`RefsFable F8`, which stands: there a
  static's `r + 1` is an arbitrary header word, left out of the
  guarantee though nothing writes it), and it only strengthens them.
