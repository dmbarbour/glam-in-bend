# Demand — 2026-11-11

A design question raised by the maintainer (2026-11-11): "We'll also need
an eval strategy suitable for copying and fusion. Currently, IIUC, demand
stops when we have a principal port facing the the interface or cursor.
Demand cannot enter a port with a positive principal (ap). Consequently,
demand stops on a bind (lam) and on some control nodes. A viable tweak is
instead to have demand continue following through a lam or control node
(i.e. demand flows through negative aux?) to support partial eval of
function bodies. A related question is how demand should flow through a
fan (merge, IIUC), or what that would even mean. Discuss, contextualize,
advise, perhaps have Fable offer a second opinion."

Two reviewers answer independently: the agent, here, and a Fable
sub-agent, whose opinion is kept as written in
[`DemandFable_2026-11-11.md`](DemandFable_2026-11-11.md). The last
section compares them. This review proposes; the maintainer decides.

## What demand does today

The ports by polarity are listed in `spike/rules.bend`:
- Fn is `[+ fn, - result, + arg]`;
- Ap is `[- ap, - arg, + result]`;
- Copy is `[- input, + left, + right]`;
- Merge is `[+ out, - left, - right]`;
- Lift is `[- in, + out]`, facing the source;
- LiftC is `[+ out, - in]`, facing the consumer.

Every wire joins a positive port to a negative one, and values flow from
positive to negative.

The walk in `spike/kdemand.blend` begins at the interface and follows
principal ports. Each node on its path was entered by an output, a
positive port. From there it follows the node's negative principal to its
peer:
- **The peer is a positive principal:** that is an active pair, and it is
  rewritten.
- **The peer is a positive auxiliary:** that node must reduce first, so
  it is pushed and the walk goes on from it.

So the walk passes only through nodes whose principal is negative: Ap,
Op, Copy, Lift. A positive principal is a value: Fn, Data, Merge, LiftC,
EraP, FnD. The walk stops when the interface faces one, except that a
LiftC there moves past by the root's rule.

**A correction to the question's wording.** Ap's principal is negative,
so demand does enter Ap: through its result, then on to the function it
applies. The nodes with positive principals are the values Fn, Merge
and LiftC. Demand stops at them only at the interface, since inside the
net a positive principal on the walk's path is half of a pair. The
question's point stands, with that correction: demand stops at a value
at the root, and goes no further into a lambda's body.

**Scoping keeps demand going backward** (`net-scoped-arguments`, raised
by the maintainer, 2026-11-11). A function's argument may be used only
within its body: every path from the exposed port to the argument passes
through the function's result. `interaction_net` checks this with a
dominator tree, and glam's lowering never breaks it.

So demand only ever steps backward, from an output to the inputs it
needs. Entering an Ap through its result and going on to the function
is such a step. The one forward step would be entering a Fn by its
argument, and in a scoped net it happens only after copying, where
scoping holds per sharing context rather than by structure. Demand
reports it as state 7.

Three things rest on this. The collector follows inputs only. Body
demand below reaches a function's own variable only from inside its
body. And in a live template's source, pending work reaches the
parameter only through the body, so positive cursors sit only at ports
the result dominates.

## Demand into a value's negative auxiliaries

The proposed tweak continues the walk, past a value reached by its
positive principal, into that value's negative auxiliary ports. Kind by
kind:

| Reached | Its negative auxiliaries | What continuing means |
|---|---|---|
| Fn, FnD | `result`, the body | reduction under the binder |
| LiftC | `in` | looking through a level control to the value behind it |
| Merge | `left`, `right` | each copy context's version of a shared value |
| Data, EraP | none | nothing |

**Through a Fn,** the walk reduces the body toward a head normal form. It
stops at one of three places:
- a value at the body's head;
- the function's own variable, a neutral head (`\x -> x a`): today state
  7, "a variable demanded outside its function's body", which here is
  inside and fine;
- the variable of an enclosing function.

Going on into a neutral head's arguments (an Ap's negative `arg`) would
be full normal form.

**Through a LiftC,** the walk sees a value through a control, as the
root's rule already does at the interface. Inside a body this is
needed: otherwise a value behind a control stops the walk early.

**Through a Merge,** the walk would demand every context's version of a
shared value. A merge's principal facing up is a superposition. The
consumer above faces it with an auxiliary port, and it resolves only
when that consumer's own principal meets something and the merge, facing
it principal to principal, commutes through it. Demanding both sides is
work for contexts that may never demand it: a spark, never demand.
Inside the net, demand reaching a merge from below is always half of a
pair (the commutation), so the question arises only where the walk
starts from an auxiliary port: at the root, where a superposition reads
as none, at a cursor, or under body demand. In each case the merge is a
finished value for the contexts to resolve, and the walk stops there.

**A Copy fan** is already right. Demand reaching it by an output follows
its negative input to the shared value and reduces it once, for both
outputs.

**What body demand costs and risks:**
- **It does not terminate.** A recursive call under the binder is a
  need on a template's leaf. Serving it unfolds the recursion without
  end, as HeapModel says: "Local reduction before freezing reduces
  build-only pairs only, never calls (reducing a call there could unroll
  recursion without end)".
- **It does work no copy may need,** such as both branches of a choice
  under a lambda.
- **It may meet a reflection task,** which early evaluation may not emit.

So body demand is not demand. It is early evaluation: an optimization
that never changes an outcome, stopped by fuel and by reflection tasks
(`early-evaluation-optimization`). `net-demand-whnf` admits it as a
strategy "guaranteed to terminate", the fuel being the guarantee.

## Live templates need positioned demand

A copy's negative cursor names a position in the source: a finished
node's negative auxiliary port, facing a positive auxiliary port of an
unfinished node. Demand at the cursor is today's walk, started there
rather than at the interface. It ends in one of these ways:
- **A value:** a positive principal now faces the position, and that
  structure is materialized into the copy.
- **The frontier:** the walk reaches a finished node's positive
  auxiliary port, such as the function's variable or a fan's output
  under it. That is state 7 reinterpreted as "frontier reached,
  materialize", and positive cursors are joined there.
- **A need:** served in the source, by the same task.
- **Stuck, a cycle, or out of fuel or memory:** as today.

So "demand through a lambda" happens under live templates without any
new rule. Each copy demands a body only when it needs one, through a
cursor, and the source reduces that part once for every copy: lazy
partial evaluation, one position at a time.

## Proposed: three strategies

1. **Demand to weak head normal form at the root,** unchanged
   (`net-demand-whnf`).
2. **Positioned demand,** the same walk started at a cursor. It stops at
   a value, the frontier, a need, stuck, a cycle, or the runtime's
   limits. It is needed by `cells-freeze.cursors`.
3. **Body demand,** a bounded strategy for early evaluation. It walks
   into the negative auxiliaries of Fn, FnD and LiftC, never of Merge.
   It reduces the pairs on its path under fuel, leaves needs unserved by
   default (or serves them within a budget), and stops before a
   reflection task. It runs:
   - on a source before its first copy, to grow the finished part and
     leave fewer cursors (the maintainer's "drive source via demand
     before copying anything at all");
   - when a source is frozen whole;
   - as sparks, later.

**With fusion.** Body demand on a source before any copy fuses freely,
since nothing is marked yet. Positioned demand after copies exist meets
marked controls, which fusion refuses (`copied-controls-unfused`).
Passing through a marked LiftC moves the walk without fusing it.

## Experiments that decide

- **Body demand as a strategy** in `spike/kstrat.bend`, run on the
  library: how many pairs under binders reduce within a fuel of k, 2k
  and 4k a node, and how much of each source becomes finished.
- **On the sharing families:** cursors and joins per copy, with and
  without body demand before the first copy.
- **Needs served under a budget, against left alone,** on recursive
  samples (`hello_recursion.g`, `hello_mutual.g`), to bound the
  unfolding.

## Comparison with the second reviewer

The two agree on the shape: three strategies sharing one walk, differing
in where they start and where they stop.
- **Demand to weak head normal form** stays the semantics.
- **Positioned demand** starts at a cursor.
- **Bounded head demand** is an optimization under
  `early-evaluation-optimization`. It goes through Fn, FnD and LiftC,
  never into a merge's sides or an Ap's argument, so it computes head
  normal form and never full normal form. It leaves needs unserved and
  stops before a reflection task.

Both find that demand never reaches a merge inside a net, because the
consumer's rule distributes over it. Demanding both sides is
speculation, a spark at most.

**What the second reviewer adds:**
- **Controls do not stop demand.** A LiftC at the interface is crossed
  by the root's rule; a Lift on the path is walked through; every pair
  of a control with a base kind has a rule. What looks like a stop at a
  control is the lift the lowering puts under a variable, which the
  walk passes through to the variable, ending in state 7.
- **The common prefix.** Beta links an Ap's result peer to the Fn's
  result peer and its argument peer to the Fn's variable peer
  (`rule.ap KFn`). So the walk to weak head normal form of any demanded
  application, after the beta, is the walk head demand makes from the
  result port, as far as the variable. Head demand under a binder thus
  reduces only pairs that every demanded application of the function
  would reduce. It is wasted only on a function never applied and
  demanded.

  This makes head demand on a function-valued cell's net, when its
  task ends and before its first copy, the natural place to run it.
  There fusion is still free and no mark is set, and it turns per-copy
  work into source work, which is the maintainer's "drive source via
  demand before copying anything at all". In `\x -> (\y -> y) x` the
  pair becomes a wire, and in `\x -> (g c) x`, with `g c` closed, it
  becomes finished structure, with no cursor left.
- **Termination needs one more stop.** `f y = f (g y)` has a weak head
  normal form and no head normal form, so head demand would splice
  `f`'s own template under its binder without end. It stops at a splice
  of the source's own template, or at a splice budget, besides fuel.
- **The frontier is wider than the variable.** After a beta relinks
  peers, a pending node can enter a finished Copy or Lift by its output
  and climb through it to the variable. So positioned demand's frontier
  is "a finished node entered by an output", found by scanning the path
  at the stop, as `fw.scan` already scans for state 7. This corrects
  this review's "the function's variable or a fan's output under it".
- **The scan needs a finished set per source,** not a header bit. A
  bitmap, a word for 32 nodes as the collector's marks are, is built by
  the finished walk and extended at each materialization. Fans and
  functions use a form bit for being unlevelled, and data uses both
  form bits, so only controls have a free bit
  (`copied-controls-unfused` relies on that).
- **A superposition at the interface becomes an outcome of its own,**
  as a void did (`net-void-demanded`), rather than a value with no
  data.

**Where they differ.** This review allowed needs to be served within a
budget; the second reviewer would never serve them in a body pass,
leaving each to become a cursor served under real demand. The second is
simpler and agrees with "serve needs only under real demand"; adopted
as the proposal, with a budget as an experiment at most.

**Experiments,** merging both lists:
1. **The finished fraction.** On the sharing families: finished nodes,
   negative and positive cursors, with and without head demand before
   the first copy. Head demand earns its keep if `closed_work.g`'s 147
   interactions a use fall toward the 19 it costs linked, with no
   cursor left.
2. **The common prefix,** on random terms: head demand, then apply and
   demand, against apply and demand alone. The values must agree, and
   the interactions be no more than the fans' distribution adds.
   Disagreements are shrunk into `spike/library.txt`.
3. **Divergent and strict heads** as library entries: `\x -> f (g x)`
   with `f y = f (g y)`, which must stop by the splice stop; and a body
   whose head is a strict call on the variable, which stops at once.
4. **A superposition at the root,** sought in the differential store and
   the ladder.
5. **Marked controls under head demand** run on a source that already
   has copies.

**Questions for the maintainer,** merged:
1. Head demand as an optimization only, with weak head normal form the
   semantics, so that a function with no head normal form stays a value?
2. Where does its fuel come from? An annotation, a global budget, the
   Rust bootstrap's 4 reductions a node up to 256 nodes, or a heuristic
   tuned from profiles as `bounded-duplication` allows?
3. Should a body pass leave needs unserved?
4. Should a superposition at the interface be an outcome of its own?
5. Is a finished bitmap per source, scanned at the stop, an acceptable
   frontier test?
6. Sparks on a fan's other side: now, or after cursors?
