# Decision records: the why, not the when

A cleanup review of `docs/decisions/` (`cursor-laws.records`), at the
maintainer's request (2026-11-24): "architecture decision records aren't
the right place for ordering decisions. Those are primarily supposed to
represent the why of decisions, not the when. The when is the role of
plans and git history."

## Method

All 74 records were read by a sub-agent, which classed each as clean,
mixed (its why with some ordering, scheduling or status besides), or
mostly ordering, quoting what it found. Its quotes were checked against
the files by a script, and five of them again by the agent writing this
review. The findings below summarize its quotes. Line numbers are
approximate.

- Clean: 28.
- Mixed: 35.
- Mostly ordering: 1.
- Borderline: 10, records whose standing rules carry a condition
  ("until", "once") that reads as a design rule rather than a schedule.

## Proposed treatment

These rules are put to the maintainer before any record is edited (Q1).

- **R1. When-content leaves the record.** Sequencing ("first, then"),
  deferrals ("for now", "until step X", "later"), routes taken first and
  status reports (built, merged, measured on a date) are removed. Git
  history keeps them. An item still open with no other owner, such as a
  deferral nobody tracks, moves to an active plan step or to a plan's
  Open list, named there.
- **R2. A measurement stays only as a reason.** A cost that motivated
  the decision stays: fusion off everywhere costing 8 to 24%
  (`copied-controls-unfused`), templates against flags costing 4 to 15%
  (`generated-sources`). One that confirms afterwards that nothing
  regressed goes, its run id with it (`drops-stop-at-index`' run 319,
  `copied-controls-unfused`' run 321, `splice-erasers-static`' runs 326
  to 328).
- **R3. "Rule lives in" names a place.** Timing in those lines goes:
  "with its merge", "on the side branch", "until built".
- **R4. A title states the decision.** Titles carrying a schedule are
  restated: `serve-premises-over-index` ("to be reviewed for laws
  later"), `store-collection` ("stop-the-world, then SATB").
- **R5. Attribution stays.** "The maintainer, 2026-11-13: ..." dates a
  ruling, not a schedule.
- **R6. `rev` stays.** Removing when-content does not revise a decision,
  as a typo fix does not (`docs/Decisions.md`).

## Findings

### F1 `wiring-premise-before-refinement`: mostly ordering

"the runtime checks kept and `noself` left for later" (L22); "`sv.front.c`'s
check stays until the premise is discharged" (L33); "until it is, a loaded
net's wiring is itself a named premise" (L40). Its why: the premise is the
audit's own verdict (`KN.wired_ok`), so wired has one definition, and
`wired_port` ties it to the per-port reading; the draft refinement cannot
hold as drafted. Proposed: the record keeps that why, retitled (R4) to the
decision, for example "A net's wiring is the audit's verdict"; the route
and timing are already in `docs/plans/KernelRefine_2026-11-24.md`.

### F2 to F36: mixed

Each keeps its why. What would leave it, by R1 to R3:

- **F2 `builtin-imports-compiler-local`**, close to mostly ordering: "the
  hello ladder defers module imports until then" (L7), "may be supported
  before modules become objects" (L14), "`ladder-effects` need not wait"
  (L18). Stays: built-in imports belong to the compiler and only look
  like imports.
- **F3 `copied-controls-unfused`**: "as checked when it was built" (L28),
  run 321 (L35), "Instruction-level comparisons come later" (L42). Stays:
  copied controls are marked so fusion cannot change one a copy took;
  the 8 to 24% reason.
- **F4 `copy-reduced-function-nets`**: the quick start's history (L48),
  "Live templates are built now" (L52). Stays: a live template freezes
  finished parts and leaves unfinished ones to reduce once, by cursors.
- **F5 `corruption-reported-when-cheap`**: the ring's walks "before the
  index replaced them" (L21), the join's self-check "no law yet
  excludes" (L28) with its docket quote. Stays: correctness by proof; a
  state only corruption reaches is reported where detecting it is free.
  Note: the self-join check's law now exists under premises
  (`front_joins`), which this record should not track either.
- **F6 `cursors-indexed`**, heavy history: built on a side branch
  (L23), laws retiring at a merge (L66), "Option C ... waits until it
  can be exercised usefully" (L74), store run 340 (L53). Stays: a

  moves to a plan as an open thread (R1).
- **F7 `drop-runs-fuse`**: "the kernel's part waited for 'a workload
  that shows drops piling up'" (L9), the order of model and laws (L15).
