# Allocation and the heap's growth under the laws

Scope. `cursor-laws.alloc` (`docs/plans/CursorLaws_2026-11-23.md`): how
the laws manage the free list and the heap's growth, prompted by
`LM.ext.ok` (`glam/loop_model.bend:37`), the eight premises under which
`lx_extend` and `serve_next` grow the loop's tracked nodes. The
maintainer (2026-11-26): "Adding six premises related to free lists
suggests it's about time to lawfully manage allocation and growth
explicitly. But I'm not sure exactly when to handle it and how much it
cleans up beyond the immediate." Two reviewers: this one, the agent's,
and a Fable sub-agent's, kept as written beside it
(`AllocFable_2026-11-26.md`), compared below. Read: the kernel's
allocator and growth (`spike/kprim.bend` 160-600, `spike/kmem_grow.bend`
55-105), the free list's three models (`spike/freelist.bend`,
`spike/kplace.bend`'s growth, `spike/kmodel.bend`'s `chain`, `apart`,
`past`, `glam/index_model.bend`'s `fl.ok`), the laws over them
(`spike/LAWS.bend`'s `free_list_*` and `grow_*`, the splice's, the
loop's), the record `splice-frame-premises` and the Cells plan's
`cells-freeze.nested.host.alloc`, `glam/ring_model.bend`'s `px.ok`
parts. Laws' premises counted by pattern, not read one by one. Nothing
built or run.

## Findings

**F1. `LM.ext.ok` is the allocator's prefix law, met again as
premises.** The record `splice-frame-premises` (2026-11-19) already
holds the target, the second reviewer's then: "one prefix law, 'the free
list's first k nodes are distinct, laid out apart, past the header,
below the capacity, and none live', which discharges the splice's
premise, the serve's that the ring's nodes lie past the header, and
`.host.path`'s that fresh nodes miss the walk's path, together; `FL`,
the free list's model in Nat, is the model it refines." `LM.ext.ok`'s
conjuncts are that law's facts for the nodes `E` past the tracked
prefix: the chain (1) and distinct (2) are "the free list's first k
nodes, distinct"; none of the live nodes, `s` or the index's (3, 4) is
"none live", for the loop's live set; laid out apart (5), each its own
base (7), past the header (8). "Below the capacity" is absent because
`RG.layout` states apartness mod 2^d directly (F4). The sixth premise
alone is not the allocator's: no live node's faced port linking into
`E` (6) is the wiring (F7). So the maintainer's sense is borne out, and
the target is decided; what is open is its form and its time.

**F2. Three models of the free list, unbridged.**
- `FL` (`spike/freelist.bend`), slots in `Nat`: "each free slot is on
  the free list exactly once, and nothing else is", with laws for an
  allocation, a free and growth (`spike/LAWS.bend` 428-452,
  `free_list_grow` 705, `grow_has` 746, "the free list then holds at
  least the n slots grown, what the kernel's placement needs"). Never
  stated of the heap's words.
- `KMd.chain` (`spike/kmodel.bend:85`), the words: a prefix read from
  the head, each link naming the next node. No exactness: it says
  nothing of the nodes it does not reach, nor that a node on it is not
  in use.
- `IM.fl.ok` (`glam/index_model.bend:473`): the index's branches to be,
  each its own node (`nd.ok`); layout only.

The splice laws say what the gap costs (`glam/SPLICE_LAWS.bend:24`):
"Not claimed: the reserve's and the doubling's paths, the list running
out; that ns are free and none live (the allocator's prefix law, to
come)." The Cells plan lists the same missing pieces under
`cells-freeze.nested.host.alloc`: "the free list's exactness, which
`FL`'s laws prove over a model, not over `P.alloc`". The bridge from
`FL` to the words is both the prefix law and part of
`kernel-refine.align`, where `FL.ok`'s one list must also take the
reserve.

**F3. `E` appears between serves because the loop tracks a prefix.**
The loop's free list `F` is a prefix: `situate_pend` takes
`KMd.chain(Con{s, F}, ...)` (`glam/LOOP_LAWS.bend:53`) for any `F`. If a
run tracked the whole free list, the chain run to its end (a head or a
last link of 0), the next copy's nodes would lie in `F` while the list
lasts. `E` would then arise only where the list runs dry, which the
kernel handles in two ways. It taps the reserve (`alloc.tap`,
`spike/kprim.bend:494`). Or it doubles the store (`grow.done`, 466):
`KM.grow` copies the words into zeros (`spike/kmem_grow.bend:59`), and
`thread` (`spike/kprim.bend:240`) writes each fresh node's word 1 to
link the next. Fresh nodes lie past the old capacity, zero but their
links. Their `LM.ext.ok` then holds by arithmetic, given that the
tracked nodes and the live links lie below the old capacity. The
untracked free nodes' facts move to a run's start, once, instead of to
every serve that outgrows the prefix.

**F4. The layout is arithmetic.** `AD.slot(i, d)` is `i mod 2^d`
(`defs.bend:34`); `RG.layout` (`glam/ring_model.bend:169`) and
`KMd.apart` are inequalities of slots. Two consequences:
- For nodes whose words lie below 2^d, distinct bases (multiples of 4,
  `RG.bases`) have disjoint words, and nodes past the header miss the
  head's word. So `layout ⟸ distinct ∧ bases ∧ past ∧ below`, every
  conjunct but distinctness a per-node fold.
- Layout at depth d gives layout at d + 1: words distinct mod 2^d are
  distinct mod 2^(d+1). The doubling keeps the old nodes' layout, and
  fresh nodes are apart from the old ones when the old lie below 2^d.

This is `bases_apart`, generalized. With it, growing the tracked list
(premise 5) is a question of distinctness alone, and the splice's
`KMd.apart` premise follows the same way.

**F5. A free node is marked in its words, apparently.** `free` zeroes
the node's header (`spike/kprim.bend:581`, `KM.m_set(w, base(r), 0)`),
growth's fresh words are zero, and no node is written with kind 0: no
`hd2(0` or `hd(0` in `spike/` or `glam/`, and the index's branch has
kind 18 (`glam/index.bend:34`). Checked by grep, not by law. If "free"
were "header 0" as a stated invariant, then a free node's being none of
the live nodes nor the index's would hold node by node. The live and
index nodes' headers are nonzero, and that would replace the cross-list
facts (`none.in(F, L)`, `none.in(F, Con{s, IM.ix.nodes(I)})`) now
carried in `RG.px.ok`. This is a state shape in which the partition
holds by computation (P10).

**F6. Growth during a copy is outside every law.** The splice laws
cover the pop path only (F2's quotation). The serve's laws inherit
that: `hl` (`SM.made(ts) == List.length(&2, U32, ns)`), with `ns ++ F`
the chain, has no solution when the list is shorter than the copy. So
the loop's laws are silent exactly where a copy outgrows the free list.
`cursor-laws.loop.shape.grow` must choose between two courses:
- Laws over the node pass's three paths: pop, tap, double. The depth
  changes in the middle of a pass, and the splice's exact write list
  widens, which `splice-frame-premises` names a law change ("growth,
  when it enters, widens the list").
- Growth only at a serve's boundary: an ensure before each copy (a walk
  of k links, or a count of free nodes kept in the header). The
  kernel's growth would then thread its fresh nodes ahead of a nonempty
  list, as the model's `KP.grow` already does ("the last the old head",
  `free_list_grow`'s comment). That is a change to the kernel and the
  materializer, to measure; a count is on the hot path of every
  allocation and free.

**F7. Premise 6 is the no-dangling-link fact.** `po.n`
(`glam/ring_model.bend:300`): a live node's faced port's link word is
"off every word of a tracked node that is not live". Over `E` that is:
no live port links a free node. With F5's mark, that reads "every live
port links a node in use", which the wiring premise
(`net-obvious-errors`) gives, the kernel refinement's to discharge, as
already accepted.

**F8. The count, and what cleaning up means.** By pattern, about 49
laws take a premise touching the free list. The categories overlap, and
the free head's pattern also catches a header fact (`hhd`):
- the chain: 9 (index 4, splice 4, loop 1);
- `IM.fl.ok`: 13 (glam's `LAWS.bend` 10, index 3);
- the free head's word: 27;
- `RG.px.ok`, `LM.lx.ok` or `LM.ext.ok`, which carry the list: 23
  (`LAWS.bend` 12, serve 6, loop 5).

Few of them need restating. The laws take these premises as building
blocks, and a prefix law that concludes the shapes they take
(`KMd.chain`, `RG.layout` or `KMd.apart`, `KMd.past`, `IM.fl.ok`,
`none.in`) discharges them where laws compose: a run's start, growth,
the serve's composition. The exception is growth inside a copy (F6),
which changes the splice's statements.

## Approaches

Each approach is listed with what it discharges, whether that holds by
computation, its proof cost and its kernel cost.

- **A. Premises as now.** Discharges nothing. Free to keep.
  `LM.ext.ok` stays the loop's interface either way: the other
  approaches prove it from below, by laws concluding `LM.ext.ok`,
  rather than restating the laws that take it.
- **B. Whole-list tracking.** Moves premises 1-4, 7 and 8 from every
  serve that outgrows the prefix to a run's start (F3).
  - By computation: no.
  - Proofs: none restated. The run's law, still to come, instantiates
    `F` as the whole list and adds the chain's end.
  - Kernel: none.
- **C. Layout by arithmetic** (`bases_apart`, generalized). Discharges
  premise 5, the splice's `KMd.apart`, and the layout premises of the
  serve's laws, from per-node facts and distinctness (F4).
  - By computation: yes, but for distinctness.
  - Proofs: a family of lemmas over `Nat.mod`, small. A per-node
    "below 2^d" fact enters where the doubling needs it.
  - Kernel: none.
- **D. The free list exact over the words.** `FL.ok` refined to the
  heap: the chain from the head reaches 0 through distinct nodes, each
  free, in range and past the header, and the reserve's chain likewise.
  Discharges premises 1-4, 7 and 8 at a run's start, the splice's "ns
  free, none live", and the reserve's tap.
  - By computation: in part, with E.
  - Proofs: substantial. Every writer must keep it: allocation, free,
    growth, every rewrite (`free_list_*` over the words; the spike's
    laws exist in `Nat`).
  - Kernel: none. It is `kernel-refine.align`'s work as well.
- **E. Header 0 as the free mark** (F5). Turns "none live" and "none of
  the index's" into per-node header facts.
  - By computation: yes, once established.
  - Proofs: a law that every node written has a kind of at least 1, and
    a restatement of `RG.px.ok`'s free-list conjuncts if they are to
    go. Those touch about 23 laws, so later, if ever. Within D it costs
    little.
  - Kernel: none.
- **F. A high-water mark for nodes**, as the data table keeps one (its
  free head beside its high-water mark, `spike/kprim.bend` 160-170).
  Fresh nodes would sit past the mark, never threaded. Discharges the
  fresh nodes' facts by arithmetic, as the doubling's threading already
  allows at the moment of growth (F3).
  - By computation: only with frame laws for every writer (nothing
    writes past the mark).
  - Proofs: moderate.
  - Kernel: the empty-list path changes: cheaper growth, no thread.
    Measure it.
  - Little gain over B with C for the laws. Consider it with D.
- **G. Growth at a serve's boundary** (F6). Keeps every serve law on
  the pop path.
  - By computation: yes, at the boundary.
  - Proofs: small. `ext_fresh`, `LM.ext.ok` for the doubling's fresh
    nodes, from C and the zeroed words.
  - Kernel: an ensure before each copy, and growth ahead of a nonempty
    list. Measure it.
  - The alternative, laws over all three paths inside a copy, costs
    more proofs and no runtime.

## When

- **Merge `cursor-laws.loop.shape.next` as it stands.** `LM.ext.ok` is
  the right interface. Every approach above proves it from below, so
  nothing on the branch would be restated.
- **Next, before `.root`: C.** It is small and independent, it
  discharges premise 5 everywhere, and the doubling needs it (F4).
- **With `.grow`: G against the three-path laws**, decided by
  measuring the ensure. `ext_fresh` lands there, with B as the run's
  instance.
- **With `kernel-refine.align`: D, with E as its notion of free.** The
  reserve is realigned there anyway, and building the exact list twice,
  once for the loop and once for the refinement, is the cost to avoid.
  Until then a run's start takes the exact list as one premise.
- **F only if D's design or a measurement favors it.**

So the cleanup beyond the immediate premises is large in what gets
discharged: the splice's fresh-node premises, the serve's, the index's
`fl.ok`, `LM.ext.ok`, and the wiring reduced to one per-link fact. It
is small in what gets restated, if the laws that do the discharging
conclude the shapes the existing laws already take.

## Questions for the maintainer

1. Should a free node be the one whose header is 0, stated as the
   kernel's invariant (every node it writes has a kind of at least 1)?
   Then "free" can be read from the heap, and a free node's being none
   of the live nodes holds node by node.
2. When a copy outgrows the free list, should growth happen only
   between serves (a check before each copy, a kernel change to
   measure), or should the laws cover growth inside a copy?
3. Should a run's laws track the whole free list, the reserve read as
   its continuation, so that its facts are premises once per run?
4. Should the free list's exactness over the heap be built with the
   kernel refinement's alignment, the reserve among it, rather than for
   the loop first?
