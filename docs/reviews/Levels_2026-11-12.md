## How a copy gets its levels

- **A source is reduced at its own levels.** A cell's recipe is lowered
  closed, at depth 0, and forced on a task of its own; its net, once a
  function, is the source. Its pending work reduces there, for every
  copy, at the source's levels.
- **A copy has one offset.** The first use sets it in the situating
  node, as a splice sets its own: the level of the application that met
  the leaf (`sp.by` in `glam/splice.bend`). Every materialization of
  that copy raises by the same amount.
- **What is raised.** `sp.node` raises the levels of functions,
  applications and fans, and the indices of controls. It leaves controls'
  amounts alone, and data and operators carry no level. A level past the
  header's 24-bit field is an error, as at load.
- **Only applications meet a use.** A leaf is data. A copy fan meeting
  it duplicates the leaf, and a control facing the source passes it
  unchanged (`rule.copy`, `rule.lift` on `KData` in `spike/rules.bend`).
  So every use is met by an application, whose level is the offset. An
  application that crossed a control met the leaf with its level already
  adjusted. The other cases are an operator, where the use is stuck, and
  the interface, at level 0. This settles where the doubt raised in the
  `cells-freeze.cells` outline came from: no use takes its offset from a
  fan or a control. Whether that offset is right after the leaf crossed
  controls as data is the second reviewer's L3, the splice premise, and
  is not settled by this.

## The reference: the expansion

A host holding copies stands for its **expansion**: the net in which each
copy's outstanding cursors are replaced by its source's unmaterialized
part, raised by the copy's offset. Cursors are right if:
- every expansion is a net the host could reach by splicing the recipe
  at that application and reducing in place, the stopgap built today;
- the host's steps, the source's steps, serves and joins each keep the
  host and its expansion in step.

## What must hold

1. **L1, shift.** Raising both nodes' leveled fields by k, then applying
   the rule, gives what the rule gives raised by k. The table's own tests
   must not change under the raise:
   - an application's level against a function's (`lvl_eq`);
   - two fans' levels (`fan_eq`);
   - two controls' indices (`ctl_eq`).

   Two pieces of arithmetic must also commute with the raise, given no
   overflow: a level through a control (`lift_lvl`) and fusion's
   `compose`. The same goes for events and the kernel's level checks.

   This is the property `templates-spliced-at-level` rests on today,
   with differential evidence only. It is a law over the table, in this
   repository's style: a case split over kinds and specs, with a Nat
   lemma `CT.raise(n + k, b, j + k) = CT.raise(n, b, j) + k`, through the
   existing U32-to-Nat bridge (`lift_raise` in `spike/LAWS.bend`). A
   croissant lowers by 1 only, and only levels above its index, so
   truncated subtraction never shows. **Bend; the most valuable single
   law.**
2. **L2, the copier raises uniformly.** Materializing at offset k gives
   the materialization at 0 raised by k, and so does freezing a raised
   net. Raises compose, by a then by b being by a + b, which nested copies
   use: a copy of a source that itself holds a copy. Overflow is an error,
   never a crash. This is over `sp.node` and `fz.node.c`: unit tests, and
   a small law over a template node's raise.
3. **L3, locality.**
   - A source's pairs hold source nodes only.
   - Host nodes meet only materialized nodes, never a source's.
   - Every pair with a cursor waits; the kinds law already shows it
     stuck.
   - A materialized node equals its original raised, since the original
     never changes (`copied-nodes-untouched`, and
     `copied-controls-unfused` for fusion).

   Mostly structural, and largely in hand.
4. **L4, commutation.** A source's step, taken before a copy's host steps
   and at the source's levels, gives what the same step gives in the
   expansion at the copy's offset, interleaved with the host's. It
   follows from L1, L3 and one-step confluence, since disjoint pairs
   commute. A theorem over reductions rather than over the table, so
   Lean, or a Bend model of small nets.
5. **L5, the interface.** A net's interface takes the place of a control
   facing it (`rule.root`). That is right where a net's value is read,
   and not obviously right where the value is applied. In the expansion,
   the control would meet the host's application, pass it, and go on into
   its argument and result (`rule.ap` on `KLiftC`). If a source's
   interface ever absorbs a control in front of a function, its copies
   lose that control. What to state:
   - in a net lowered from a closed term, no control faces the interface
     ahead of a function;
   - or else, a source's interface keeps such controls, and the first
     serve materializes them with the root.

   Probed below; not settled.
6. **L6, limits.** Offsets add up through nested copies, so the 24-bit
   field bounds nesting times depth. Past it is an error, as the splice
   reports, inherited by the materializer through `sp.nodes`.

## Evidence gathered (probes, not committed)

Temporary probes drove the core as `glam` does, intercepting each cell
whose net ends done. The programs: the hello samples and the sharing
families, plus 82 written for this. In each, `g` is a function-valued
cell built through boxes (self-application, Church numerals, functions
returned from arguments), used once or many times at depths 0 to 2,
some passing Church numerals through it. The files are in the session's
scratchpad.

- **Every function-valued cell ends at level 0.** All 36 such cells
  probed, samples included, end at WHNF with a function at level 0
  facing the interface. Whether a control was absorbed on the way was not
  observed (L5).
- **Copied whole, the reduced source agrees.** Each cell's reduced net
  was read back whole (`F.freeze`) and spliced at every later use in
  place of its recipe:
  - reduced to its value only, all 112 programs tried write the same
    bytes as the recipe spliced and reduced in the host (exit codes
    aside: 25 programs the build refuses);
  - reduced fully first (`KS.reduce_h`, under its lambdas too, as a
    source behind cursors is), 92 of 96 agree. The other four are
    `partial_in_cell`, whose source needs templates that eager
    reduction cannot splice, a limit of the probe.

  Fully reduced sources cost fewer interactions in the host, 274
  against 546 in the deepest case.
- **The check sees levels but not controls.**
  - Raising every level of the spliced copy by 1 fails all 64
    multi-use programs.
  - Raising every control's index by 1 changes none of 80, and flipping
    every control's direction none of 64.

  The controls left in these copies are inert: what passes through them
  never sits at a level they move. So the evidence covers functions',
  applications' and fans' levels, and leaves controls to L1 and to
  programs built to make them matter.

What the probes do not test: piecewise materialization, cursors, joins,
waits at cursors, sources needing other cells. The ladder's sharing
check and `glam/test_materialize.bend` cover a little of that, without
levels.

## GAL over polarization

The cursor-specific obligations, L2, L3 and L5, are small. L1 and L4 are
the general GAL facts that templates spliced at level already assume. If
the expansion is a reachable net, cursors add nothing to GAL's own
correctness.

That correctness is NetPairing's `pairing-lean`: Guerrini's properness,
rules preserving it, and a proper net's reduction simulated on its
unfolding. Two things to start from: a copy meets only deeper nodes, and
fans meeting at one level are residuals of one duplication.

Polarity and scoping, the dominator check of `net-scoped-arguments`,
enter as preconditions:
- polarity fixes which way values flow, so a box is well defined
  (`pairing-nets`);
- scoping keeps a variable's consumers inside its function's body. That
  is what makes them finished nodes of a source, and makes a forward
  step, state 7, mean the frontier in positioned demand.

A formal core covering both general confidence and cursors would hold:
- polarized nets and the rule table, as data;
- the dominance condition;
- levels with the shift action of L1;
- properness, then the simulation.

The agent's suggested order:
1. **L1 in Bend, now.** It fits the existing laws and gives
   `templates-spliced-at-level` its first proof.
2. **L5 settled by a probe, then fixed if needed.** Count controls the
   interface absorbs over the library and the samples, and build a case
   where one faces it.
3. **L2 as tests** with `.cells`: a materialization at k against a whole
   splice at k, node for node.
4. **A differential of reduced sources in the ladder,** the probe above
   made permanent: every function-valued cell's reduced net copied whole
   against its recipe, with programs whose controls matter.
5. **L4 and properness in Lean** under `pairing-lean`, time-boxed, with
   L1's statement shared.

## Second reviewer

The second review reaches the same structure by another path. The agent
checked its claims about the code: the kernel keeps a leveled fan's level
and drops its site (`fan_hd.go` in `spike/knet.bend`); a drop's
subtraction in `lift.go` is unguarded and `lift_wraps` tests raises only
(`spike/kprim.bend`); the materialize test's functions carry no level.

**Agreed.**
- Shift equivariance of the table first, in Bend: this review's L1, the
  second's L1, which adds `copy_fn_bad` to the checks and notes that
  `lift_wraps` commutes only below the cap.
- One offset per copy, taken from the application that meets the leaf;
  offsets compose by addition along chains of copies.
- Position stability, payloads included: the control mark is not built
  while positioned demand still fuses.
- Order independence and the composed net as theorems for Lean, or a
  model checked on families in Bend.
- Bounds: raises are checked before they are written.

**What the second review adds.**
- **The splice premise (its L3).** A leaf passes a control unchanged and
  is then spliced at the applying level. That equals the template
  flowing through the control only if the control's index lies below
  every level of the template, Lamping's "a box's content lies deeper
  than its door". It holds as lowered; whether every rule keeps it is
  GAL's own question, older than cursors. This review had taken it for
  settled.
- **Fans pair by level alone (its L7).** With sites dropped, two fans at
  one level that are not residuals of one duplication annihilate with
  no error. A wrong value is the only symptom, and a mispairing that
  still yields the right value is invisible. No run has checked fan
  identities across a splice. Its E5, a test build keeping each fan's
  site and instance aside and asserting them at `Copy >< Merge`, is the
  one cheap way to see it: the maintainer's doubt about GAL in general,
  made testable.
- **The unguarded drop (its L8, E7).** A drop by more than the distance
  from its index underflows and is truncated into the field silently.
  It is unreachable from `.g`, where every drop is a unit, but a guard
  or a law is cheap.
- **Tests:** levels and controls in `glam/test_materialize.bend` (E1);
  random terms with closed subterms made definitions, through cells
  against the evaluator (E3), the splice premise's first random
  evidence; the raise's mutations kept as a list (E4).

**What this review adds.**
- **The interface (L5).** A net's interface absorbs a control, which is
  right for a value read and not obviously for a value applied.
- **The probes.** The reduced source copied whole agrees with the recipe
  on every program tried. This is E2's first half, done cheaply. The
  mutations (E4) found the samples blind to controls: their copies'
  controls are inert. A part no sample catches needs a sample before a
  proof, as the second review says.

**Recommended together, in order:**
1. **In Bend now:** L1, shift equivariance of the table, and the drop

2. **With `.cells`:** levels and controls in the materialize tests (E1);
   L2 as unit tests of the raise; programs whose controls matter, such
   as functions crossing boxes inside the copy, added to the sharing
   families; the raise's mutation list (E4).
3. **The interface:** count the controls a net's interface absorbs over
   the library and the samples; if any stand ahead of a function, keep
   them in a source.
4. **The splice premise and pairing:** random programs with extracted
   definitions (E3), run under the fan-identity build (E5).
5. **The oracle (E2)** as a model over the kernel, then L4 on the wiring
   model.
6. **Lean, under `pairing-lean`:** properness, its preservation, the
