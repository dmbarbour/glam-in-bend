# GAL levels across cursors: a second review

Written by a Fable sub-agent on 2026-11-12 at the agent's request, on what would have to hold for GAL levels to be right across cursors, and what a formalization would be; kept as written.

Claims about the repository carry their path; inferences are marked. "Source" is a live template's net `S`; a "copy" `C` is what a host `H` holds of it, raised by one offset `k`; a "serve" materializes a newly finished tree of `S` into `C` (`glam/materialize.bend`, header). "Level" means the field of a bind or fan, "index" the field of a control (`spike/rules.bend`, `leveled`).

## 1. What must hold

The design (`copy-reduced-function-nets`, `templates-spliced-at-level`) rests on one idea: a closed net reduced at its own levels, then raised by `k`, is the same net as one raised by `k` and then reduced. Cursors add that the raising happens piecewise, in an order the host decides, with host nodes waiting at the seams. The properties below split that idea into pieces that can each be stated and checked. L1, L2, L4 and L8 suit this repo's Bend proofs; L3 and L5 to L7 are theorems over reductions.

**L1. Shift equivariance of the rule table** (a law over `spike/rules.bend`, `spike/val.bend`, `spike/kprim.bend`). Define `shift k` on payloads: `PLvl n` to `n + k`, `PLift{n, up, by}` to `{n + k, up, by}`, `FanId{site, inst, l}` to `{site, inst, l + k}`, anything else unchanged. Then for every pair of kinds `a`, `b` and payloads `pa`, `pb`:
- the payload test is invariant: `N.rule_test(a, b, shift pa, shift pb) == N.rule_test(a, b, pa, pb)` (`same_lvl` compares form and field, `spike/kprim.bend:764`; the shift keeps the form);
- each new node's payload commutes with the shift: `V.lift_pay(shift pa, shift pb) == shift (V.lift_pay(pa, pb))`, which reduces to `lift_lvl(n + k, up, by, j + k) == lift_lvl(n, up, by, j) + k` (`spike/val.bend:698`), true because `j + k > n + k` iff `j > n`; the kernel's `P.lift` likewise (`spike/kprim.bend:783`);
- fusion and peeling commute: `V.compose` and `P.compose` test `n2 == n1 + b1` (`spike/val.bend:761`, `spike/kprim.bend:1136`), invariant under a common shift; `P.peel.amt` reads amounts only (`spike/kprim.bend:1290`);
- the level checks commute: `copy_fn_bad` compares two fields (`spike/kprim.bend:1110`); `lift_wraps` does not commute, since `j + k + by` may pass the cap where `j + by` does not, so the law holds below the cap and L8 covers the cap;
- events read no level (`events_lift_leveled` in `spike/LAWS.bend` says their specs lift only leveled kinds; the events themselves, `spike/rules.bend:771-885`, use only `SFanA` and `SNone`, no lift).

Why: the source reduces at its levels while every copy sits at its own; without L1, "reduce then copy" and "copy then reduce" differ at a single rewrite. What breaks: raise the levels of binds but not the indices of controls, and a copy at offset 2 keeps a croissant at index 0 beside binds at 2; a control the host pushes through the copy's function (`rule.lift KFn`) then crosses that croissant as a lower control and is re-indexed from 2 to 1 (`rule.lift KLiftC`, `V.lift_pay`), after which it shifts level-2 nodes it should have passed. Raise binds and not fans, and `\f -> f (f x)` copied at offset 1 keeps its copy fan at 0, where a host fan at 0 takes it for a residual of its own duplication and annihilates with it instead of commuting. The readback step's mutation, "levels read back one too high", differed on 28 of 40 programs (`docs/plans/Cells_2026-11-09.md`, `cells-freeze.cursors.readback`), which shows the ladder sees a non-uniform raise. Suits Bend: a finite split over kinds and `t`, each arm one `U32` fact of the shape `lift_raise` already proves (`spike/LAWS.bend`), stated over `N.rule_test`, `V.lift_pay`, `P.lift`, `P.compose`, `P.copy_fn_bad` by name.

**L2. One offset per copy, the one a whole splice would take** (a lemma over the materializer and the core). Every serve into `C` raises by the field of `C`'s situating node (`sv.k` in `glam/materialize.bend` reads `P.fld(hd)` of `situ`), and that field is set once, when the ring replaces the leaf, from the applying node's level as `sp.by` reads it (`glam/splice.bend`, `sp.by`; the plan's `cells-freeze.cells`, not yet built). Why: all nodes of one copy must sit in one frame. What breaks: `\x -> (\y -> \z -> y) x` applied to 7 then 8 (`glam/test_materialize.bend`): the first serve puts `Fn x` at `k1`; the second, after the inner beta, puts `Fn z` at `k2`; the host's second application is at `k1`, and `k2 != k1` leaves `Ap >< Fn` stuck by `same_lvl`, an error at best. Suits Bend: by computation, once the core makes rings; the "as a whole splice would" half is L3.

**L3. The splice premise: a handle crosses controls as its net would** (a theorem over reductions; this is where GAL's own invariant enters). A data node carries no level and passes a control unchanged (`rule.lift KData`, `spike/rules.bend:491`); the template is then spliced raised by the level of the application that meets it (`sp.by`). For that to equal what the template's net would have become had it flowed along the same wires, two things must hold at every control the handle crosses: (i) the control's index lies below every level of the template-as-net at that moment, so the control would have acted on it as a uniform shift; (ii) the application's level equals the net's root level after those shifts, which `same_lvl` at beta would demand anyway. (i) is "a box's content lies deeper than its door", Lamping's invariant, Guerrini's properness in the plan's words (`docs/plans/NetPairing_2026-10-26.md`, `pairing-lean`); on nets as lowered it holds by construction (a value made at `b + 1` meets `Br b`, then `Br (b + 1)` on levels now at least `b + 2`, and so on: `glam/lower.bend`, `use_edge.k`), and the question is whether every rule keeps it. What breaks without (i): a template with nodes at levels 0 and 1 (a function whose body applies something to an argument box) crossing a croissant at index 0 would, as a net, drop only its level-1 nodes, where the splice shifts nothing or everything; the box's fans then sit a level off and pair with the wrong partners, silently. Cursors with the current convention, every pair with a cursor waiting (`spike/rules.bend`, the `Cur` comment), add nothing to L3: a host control waits at the cursor and crosses the served value by the rules. The Joins review's second form, folding a control into the cursor's base (`docs/reviews/Joins_2026-11-11.md`, "Controls meeting a negative cursor"), is a new instance of (i) for a subnet, and should wait for the theorem. Needs Lean, or a model with reduction sequences; not a finite split.

**L4. Locality, the frontier and position stability** (lemmas over nets; the wiring model `spike/wiring.bend`). From `JoinsFable_2026-11-11.md`: (M) a rewrite relinks only its pair's external peers, so a finished node, in no pair, keeps its principal peer and every finished-to-finished wire; (F) every wire between a copied node and an uncopied one is a cursor position of that copy. For levels, add (S): nothing changes a copied node's payload in the source: its level, index or offset. Fusion was the one writer (`copied-controls-unfused`); events and the collector are the others (`copied-nodes-untouched`). Why: a serve copies the source's payload raised by `k` and joins by position; a payload changed later in the source makes the copy's level stale while later joins assume the source's. What breaks: a run fused into a copied control (`P.compose`) raises by `b1 + b2` in the source where the copy raises by `b1`; the next node joined there arrives shifted by one reckoning into a copy shifted by the other. Suits Bend: a finished predicate on the wiring model and a law per rule that a rewrite leaves it, as `rewrite_wired` is done; medium.

**L5. Order independence** (a theorem over reductions). The cursor convention delays every host pair that faces a cursor, a Lift, a Copy, an Ap or the interface, until a serve, in an order the whole-copy reduction need not take: there, the Lift would cross into the pending region first. The results agree only if the rewriting is confluent. For pairs of the rule table it is, by interaction nets' locality, one rule per pair; fusion and peeling are the parts outside that argument, and their order independence rests on the one-wire model's enumeration and the differential test (`fuse-control-runs`). Why this matters more now: the old fusion was order dependent on 8 of 600 terms, and cursors are a new reduction order. Needs Lean for a proof; the model and tests exist.

**L6. The composed-net theorem** (the cursor-specific theorem). For a host `H`, a source `S` and copies `C_i` at offsets `k_i`: serving cursors in any order, then reducing `H`, gives what copying `S` whole into `H` at each `k_i` and reducing gives; and a pending pair reduced once in `S` stands for its reduction in every copy. By induction on serves from L1 (each serve is a rewrite's worth of equivariance), L4 (the seams are exactly the cursors and nothing else moved), L5 (the delays are harmless) and L2. This is the oracle JoinsFable listed; a proof over arbitrary nets is Lean-sized, but a model over `spike/net.bend` with explicit cursors can check it by computation on families, which section 4 proposes.

**L7. Lamping's theorem for the composed net** (GAL in general). The kernel pairs leveled fans by level alone: `KN.fan_hd` drops the site (`spike/knet.bend:1185`), and there is no instantiation marker; the spike keeps site and instance and asserts they agree at one level (`spike/net.bend:769`). So correctness of sharing is exactly: two fans meeting at one level are residuals of one duplication, and a copy meets a function only at a deeper level. For a lowered term it is Lamping's theorem; for the net the kernel actually runs, a host with splices and copies, it follows only if that net is the inlined term's net up to L3 and L6. Nothing in the repo states or checks this beyond the differential test on whole terms and the four sharing families. Needs Lean; it is the maintainer's "GAL in general".

**L8. Bounds** (laws over the kernel). A raise at splice or serve is checked and reported, never wrapped: `sp.node` tests `KN.too_big(l + by)` and `too_big(i + by)` through `P.err_if` (`glam/splice.bend`), and both summands are below 2^24 whenever the template came from lowering or a freeze (a field is 24 bits, `fld_bits`), so the `U32` sum cannot wrap before the test; a hand-made template's level is not tested alone before the sum, a gap only tests can reach; a fan's raised level through `fan_too_big`; amounts are not raised, which is right. At run time `lift_wraps` and `compose`'s cap guard raises, and `lift_fits` proves the guard sufficient (`spike/LAWS.bend`). A drop is unguarded: `lift.go` computes `j - by` (`spike/kprim.bend:773`) and nothing stops `by > j`; it is safe only because lowering emits drops by 1 at an index below the levels they meet (`glam/lower.bend`, `use_edge.k`), which is L3 (i) again. A law that every drop a net holds is a unit, or a guard, would close it. Cheap in Bend.

## 2. Where the code stands

Read against L1 to L8. Nothing found contradicts the design; the gaps are things not built, one unguarded arithmetic, and a blind spot in what any test can see.

**The offset.** `sp.by` (`glam/splice.bend`, "The splice") takes the applying node's field when its kind is `KAp`, else 0. The only needs today are an application or a strict call meeting table data (`may_need`, `spike/kdemand.blend`), so a splice is raised by an application's level or, for an operator, not at all, where the operator meeting a bind is stuck anyway. For cursors, `serve` raises by the situating node's field (`sv.k` in `glam/materialize.bend`), and nothing in the core yet makes a ring: `glam/test_materialize.bend` builds one by hand at offset 0 (`ring`), with binds that carry no level (`fn()` is `PNone`), so no serve has yet run at a nonzero offset or through a control. The plan's `cells-freeze.cells` says the ring takes `sp.by` at the leaf's need. Inference: that is right for the first serve, because a leaf becomes a ring only on an application's need: a copy fan meeting a leaf shares the data node (`rule.copy KData`, `spike/rules.bend`) and a Lift passes it (`rule.lift KData`). Once the ring exists, a Lift, a copy fan or the interface facing a negative cursor is a need (`nd.cur`, `wk.root.cur` in `spike/kdemand.blend`) and is served at the ring's offset, which is L2.

**What is raised.** `sp.node` raises a bind's level (`PLvl`), a fan's level unless it is `nolvl` (`sp.raise`), and a control's index (`PLift`, `i + by`), and leaves a control's amount, an unleveled node (`bare_hd`, form `unlev`), data, operators and erasers alone. `freeze_tree` reads the same things back raw (`fz.lv`, `fz.fan`, `fz.lift` in `glam/freeze.bend`), so a serve is `freeze_tree` then `sp.nodes` at `k`: the same raise as a whole splice, node by node. This is the shape L1 wants. One asymmetry to know: a leveled fan is read back with site 0 (`fz.fan`), since the kernel keeps no site for it (`KN.fan_hd`), so a frozen template of a leveled net has no sites; harmless to the kernel, which never reads them, and a reason the spike's site check cannot be run on anything frozen.

**Bounds.** Covered in L8: raises at splice and serve are tested through `P.err_if` before the field is written, and cannot wrap first; run-time raises are guarded and the guard proved sufficient (`lift_fits`). The one hole is the drop: `lift.go` subtracts with no guard (`spike/kprim.bend:773`); `lift_wraps` tests `off_up` only. A drop is safe only while every drop is by 1 and meets a level above its index, which lowering arranges (`use_edge.k`) and `KN.load.node` does not check (it accepts any amount below 2^31). A hand-made or corrupted template with a drop by 2 at index 0 meeting level 1 would underflow to 2^32 - 1, which `P.hd` truncates to 24 bits silently. Not reachable from `.g` today; a law or a guard is cheap.

**Statics.** `sp.statics` makes every eraser of the copy static as the loader does; `fz.era` writes an eraser of the opposite polarity for a static met at a port; `sv.static.w` hands a static to the served cursor's peer. None carries a level. Erasure of a copy's function sends static erasers to its cursors (`KN.static_erase`, per JoinsFable), again level-free.

**Uses met by something other than an application.** A copy fan at a negative cursor waits and is served at the ring's offset, then duplicates the value by the rules; in the whole-copy world the fan would have entered the pending region first. Equal by L5. A Lift at a negative cursor waits likewise. The interface at a cursor is served with `at_root` (`sv.pd`, `PdValue` with `pos == iface`). A LiftC, a Merge or a value facing a positive cursor is stuck in the table, a wait by convention; the join later links the source's consumer to it and the ordinary rules run. All consistent with the first form of the Joins review; the second form, a control folded into the cursor's base, is not built and should not be until L3 (i) is shown for subnets.

**The source at its levels, the copies at theirs.** Positioned demand runs the kernel's walk without events (`pd.walk` calls `K.demand_ev(0, ...)`, `glam/position.bend`; `spike/kernel.bend:218`), but every rewrite still tries fusion: `P.fuse` is called at 22 sites in `spike/krules.bend`, and the mark of `copied-controls-unfused` is not built ("Not yet", `glam/materialize.bend` header). So today a rewrite in the source can fold a new raise into a control a copy already took, breaking L4 (S) for that control's offset. The collector's exemption (`copied-nodes-untouched`) is not built either, though no serve runs a collection yet. Both are known; they are the level-relevant half of position stability.

**Mixed forms.** `same_lvl` compares the form bits with the field (`spike/kprim.bend:764`), so an unleveled copy's function (form `unlev`) meeting a leveled host's application is stuck and reported, never paired by accident. A source and its hosts must be lowered the same way; `.g` always lowers with levels (`lowering-uses-levels`).

**Deep cursors.** A cursor inside a source, where the source itself applies another live template, is read back by `fz.node.c` as a bare `KCur` node with no payload (the `_` arm of `fz.node.f`), losing its position and ring words, and `pd.q.k` takes a cursor's principal for a value. The plan knows both (`cells-freeze.cells`, "Cursors inside a source"; `.boundary`). The level consequence, my inference: offsets compose by addition along a chain, `S` into `H` at `k1` and `H` into `H'` at `k2` putting `S`'s nodes at `k1 + k2` in `H'`. Chaining through `H`'s own cursor gets this for free, since serving `H'`'s cursor runs positioned demand in `H`, which serves `S` into `H` at `k1` and then materializes into `H'` at `k2`. Following a deep cursor straight to `S`, which the plan allows only on a proof of no lost sharing, would have to add the offsets explicitly. That answers the plan's open question on composing offsets (`cells-freeze.cursors`, "Open").

**What the checks can and cannot see.** The kernel's level checks are assertions counted as errors while the rewrite proceeds (`check_expr`, `spike/tools/gen_krules.bend:800`): an application and a function at different levels (stuck by `same_lvl`), a copy fan meeting a function at a level not below its own (`copy_fn_bad`), and a raise past the cap. The core turns any error count into a diagnostic (`out.errs`, `glam/core.bend:155`), so the ladder fails on them. What no check sees is the failure L7 names: two fans at one level that are not residuals of one duplication, which annihilate instead of commuting and give a wrong value with no error. The spike asserts site and instance agreement (`violation.lv`, `spike/net.bend:769`) but has no templates, cursors or splices (`spike/net.bend` holds none), so no run has ever checked fan identities across a splice, let alone a serve. Two copies of one source at one offset in one host carry fans with identical headers; whether they can meet is exactly L7 for composed nets.

**The second-form premise is unused.** Every pair with a cursor is stuck in the table (`spike/rules.bend`, the `Cur` comment), so nothing yet assumes that the region behind a position lies at levels no lower than the position's own. Good: that premise is L3 (i) for a subnet and should be proved, not assumed.

## 3. GAL over polarization

**What the pieces are.** Three things in the repo are one theory seen from three sides, my reading:
- *Polarity* fixes which way values flow, so the subnet that produces a value is well defined (`pairing-nets`'s sketch in `docs/plans/NetPairing_2026-10-26.md`; `docs/.tmp/polarity-and-stratification.md` makes the same point for Laurent's polarized logic: boxes are recoverable from polarity).
- *Dominance* (`net-scoped-arguments`; `docs/Design.md`, Interaction Nets) says a function's argument is reached from the exposed port only through its result. Inference: that is the condition under which the argument's uses lie inside the body's region, so the regions nest instead of overlapping; a net where it fails is one the `pairing-nets` sketch says has no levels (scope extruded). Dominance is the static entry condition of levels: it is checked once, as a net is built.
- *Levels* count the nesting; *properness* (Guerrini's, as `pairing-lean` names it) is the dynamic descendant of dominance: a net's levels agree with its regions, controls sit at region boundaries with indices below what lies inside, and every rule keeps that. After a copy, residuals share a body and the argument is scoped only per sharing context (the record's own words), which is exactly what levels track and dominance cannot.

**The minimal formal core.** Two cores, since the cursor case needs less than GAL in general.

*For cursors (L6).* Definitions: polarized nets with payloads, the rule table as data (`R.rule` already is), `shift`, a finished predicate on the wiring model, copies with cursors. Theorems: L1 (each rewrite commutes with `shift`), L4 (a rewrite relinks only external peers; finished nodes keep kind, ports and payload; every seam is a cursor), and from those plus L5 taken as a hypothesis, L6 by induction on serves. Nothing here needs properness: it is bookkeeping about offsets and seams, and it is the part cursors add. L1 and L4 fit Bend's style, finite splits by computation; L6 as a theorem over arbitrary nets does not, but as a model checked on families (section 4, E2) it gives most of the confidence at a fraction of the cost.

*For GAL in general (L3, L5, L7).* Definitions: regions and depths over a polarized net, the properness predicate `P`. Theorems: T1 lowering yields `P` (over `glam/lower.bend`'s edge rule, or `terms.py`'s); T2 every rule, fusion and peeling keep `P`, local per rule but over a region structure; T3 from `P`, a copy meets only deeper nodes and fans at one level are residuals of one duplication, Lamping's theorem for this table, which is not local and is the research part (the plan's feasibility note: weeks, and it may hold for a class of nets only); T4, the splice premise L3, that a closed proper subnet crossing a control at an index below its levels is the subnet shifted, with the pushed controls cancelling, a corollary of `P` plus confluence. L5, confluence: the table alone is an interaction-net system, so confluence is the standard diamond argument; runs and peeling simulate the unit system, which `runs_as_units` states for a run facing a non-control and enumeration covers for facing controls.

**Lean or Bend.** Bend for what is a finite split by computation: L1, L4, L8, and T1 if stated as a property of each edge lowering makes. Lean for inductions over reduction sequences on arbitrary graphs: L5, L6 as a theorem, T2 with its region structure, T3, T4. `docs/Proving.md` and `docs/BendIssues.md` say why: Bend's proofs are case splits and rewrites, with no mutual recursion and heavy costs on recursive proofs over structures; graph inductions and confluence lemmas (Newman's lemma, residual theory) are where Lean's library and tactics pay. One bridge keeps the two honest: the rule table dumped as data (`spike/tools/rules_dump.bend` exists for the switching study) can generate the Lean definitions, with a Bend law that the dump equals `R.rule`; then Lean proves about the table the kernel is generated from, not a retyped copy, which is the `generated-sources` discipline applied to proofs.

**Rough cost** (guesses, for an agent fluent in each): L1 in Bend, one to three days, on the pattern of `rules_keep_fits` and `lift_raise`; L4 on the wiring model with a finished predicate, one to two weeks; L8's drop guard and law, hours; E2's model-checked oracle, a week; Lean, polarized nets with the table and polarity preservation, days (the plan's own estimate); `P` with T1 and T2, two to four weeks; T3, open-ended, a month or more and possibly a restricted class; L5 for the table alone, days in Lean over an abstract interaction-net system, more with fusion and peeling.

## 4. Cheap evidence first

Ordered by what it buys per day. What each does not show is as important as what it does: no test here can see L7 fail when a wrong pairing still yields the right value, except E5.

**E1. Levels in the materialize tests** (`glam/test_materialize.bend`, a day). Today's three cases with binds at levels and a control: `\x -> (\y -> \z -> y) x` lowered with levels puts a croissant on `y`'s path, which the second serve must materialize at index `k` (section 1, L2's example); run at offsets 0, 1 and 2 with the host's application at the same level; one case with the copy applied inside an argument box, offset 1, and a host croissant on its result, so that a Lift waits at a negative cursor and crosses the served value; one with a copy fan waiting at a cursor. Expected: the same value and no kernel error. Shows L1 and L2 on those nets and that controls at cursors do what L5 says there. Does not touch L3 or L7.

**E2. The oracle, piecewise against whole** (a model in Bend over the kernel, a week; JoinsFable's law 6). For each family: (a) reduce the source to normal form, freeze it whole, splice at `k`, reduce the host; (b) serve on demand; (c) serve every outstanding cursor eagerly, in every order for small rings and random orders otherwise. Compare the value, the error count, and the host's final net in canonical form, as `fz.canon` compares the laws' graphs, not the value alone. Families: the three of E1; `\x -> g (h x)` (the climb); `\x -> x v v` (a fan joined); `f (g x)` across two sources; a copy duplicated by a host fan; a host with two copies of one source at one offset (`f 1 + f 2`) and at nested offsets (`f (f 1)`). Shows L1, L4, L5 and L6 on the sample, order independence included. Does not show L3, since the offset is given, nor L7 beyond what the canonical net reveals.

**E3. Random terms with definitions extracted** (one to two days). The differential test lowers whole terms only (`spike/tools/differential.sh`, `terms.py`), so the splice premise L3 has four families of evidence (`glam/sharing.txt`). Generate `.g` programs from random terms with one to three random closed subterms made top-level definitions, and run each linked and through cells, as `scripts/ladder.py` compares the samples, against the Python evaluator's value. Three hundred terms at a few sizes. Later, the same corpus with live templates on. Shows L3 on random terms, which it has never had, and L2 under real lowering; a value difference or a kernel error is a counterexample to shrink into the library. Does not show L7 (see E5).

**E4. Mutations of the raise** (hours, kept as a mutants list beside the splice). `sp.by` returning 0 or `by + 1`; levels raised but not indices; fans raised but not binds; amounts raised. Run the ladder with `--refreeze` and the sharing families on each; the readback step's "+1 on levels" mutation differed on 28 of 40, which calibrates what the samples can see. Shows which of L1's parts the current samples exercise, and which they would miss: a part no sample catches needs a sample before a proof.

**E5. Fan identities in a test build** (medium). The kernel's leveled fans carry no site or instance, so a wrong pairing is invisible unless the value changes. In a test build, keep a side array of `(site, instance)` per fan node, fresh per splice and serve, and assert at `Copy >< Merge` what the spike asserts (`violation.lv`, `spike/net.bend:769`); run E1 to E3 under it. This is the only cheap way to see L7 fail on composed nets, the maintainer's general doubt, and the one check that would find a mispairing the value hides.

**E6. Fusion across the frontier, counted** (hours, once a finished bitmap exists). Instrument `P.fuse` to count a fusion whose surviving control is finished, on the sharing families and the library, as the plan's `cells-freeze.fusion` asked before the mark was decided. Shows how often L4 (S) is at risk today; nothing about correctness.

**E7. The drop guard** (an hour). A hand template with a drop by 2 at index 0 meeting a function at level 1: by my reading of `lift.go` and `P.hd`, a silent wrap into the field today, since the pair's check is `lift_wraps`, which tests raises only; expect an error. Closes L8's hole and documents that drops are units.

Together, E1 to E4 cost under three weeks and would make L1, L2, L4 and L6 tested on every shape the design names, and L3 tested at random for the first time; E5 is the one that reaches L7. The proofs then have something to be stated over, in the order section 3 gives: L1 and L8 in Bend now, L4 next, the Lean core after the tests have stopped finding anything.
