## The join, word by word

A copy's cursors form a ring in the host's heap: each node four words,
word 3 the next member, the situating node first (`glam/materialize.bend`,
the header comment). A cursor's word 1 holds the port it faces, its one
real port, and its word 2 its position in the source; the situating
node has no ports, and word 3 is a pointer, not a port (the maintainer,
2026-11-14).

`R.join(h, x, y)` (`glam/ring.bend:166`) replaces two cursors of one
ring by a wire: it reads `u`, the port x faces (x's word 1), and `w`,
y's; links `u` and `w` (`P.link_xx`: the word `u + 1` given `w`, the
word `w + 1` given `u`, each unless static); unlinks x, then y (each
walk round the ring finds the member before, and gives that member's
word 3 the unlinked one's next); and frees x, then y (each one's word 0
cleared, its word 1 given the free list's old head, the head, word 0
of the heap, given it). `R.join1` (`:169`) is the same for a
placeholder x in no ring: no unlink of x.

An example. A ring of four: the situating node s at 8, cursors c1 at
12, c2 at 16 and c3 at 20.

| node | word 0 | word 1 (port) | word 2 (position) | word 3 (next) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| s, 8 | header | — | handle | 12 |
| c1, 12 | header | 41 | 7 | 16 |
| c2, 16 | header | 33 | 9 | 20 |
| c3, 20 | header | 48 | 5 | 8 |

c1 faces port 1 of a node at 40 (link word 42); c3 faces port 0 of a
node at 48 (link word 49). `join(h, 12, 20)` writes, in order:

1. word 42 := 48 and word 49 := 41 (the link);
2. word 11 := 16 (s, c1's predecessor, now skips it);
3. word 19 := 8 (c2, c3's predecessor, now skips it);
4. words 12, 13 and 0 (c1 freed);
5. words 20, 21 and 0 (c3 freed).

The ring left is s, c2. The words written: 0, 11, 12, 13, 19, 20, 21,
42, 49.

`join_keeps` says every word `j` reads as before, provided `j` is
outside every member's four words (`RG.outside(xs, d, j)`,
`glam/LAWS.bend:119`), is not the free list's head, and is neither
`u + 1` nor `w + 1`. Here that allows 0, 8 to 23, 42 and 49: every
word the join writes, and also words it never touches, among them
c2's port link (17) and c2's position (18). F1 is about that gap.

## F1. The words a join may write: coarser than the code

The code writes, among the ring's members, only word 3 of a predecessor
and the two joined cursors' own words; the law allows every word of
every member. That would not matter were nothing to read those words
again, but a serve does, at once. Within one materialization each
crossing of the copied tree looks its far end up in the ring
(`mz.pl`, `glam/materialize.bend:64`, calling `R.rg.find`), and the
find compares each member's word 2 with the position sought
(`rw.find.p`, `glam/ring.bend:35-37`); a crossing found is joined
(`R.join1`, `mz.found`, `:56`), so joins and finds alternate along the
crossings. In the example, were the copy's next crossing to look for
position 9, it should find c2; the law lets the join have rewritten
word 18, so a serve law built on it cannot show the find still finds
c2.

The sharper claim: `j` no member's word 3 (`RG.off.links(xs, d, j)`,
already in the model), off x's four words and off y's
(`RG.off.node`). The agent proved it in a scratch worktree with the
same lemmas and one more (`links_at`, `apart_at`'s twin), both laws,
every earlier mutation still refuted (the patch is kept in the session's
scratchpad, ready to apply).

That the sharper law catches more is shown by a mutation and a
witness, not by the mutation alone. The mutation: unlinking also clears
the predecessor's word 2 (`P.wr(h, (p + 3 : U32), nx)` made to write
`p + 2` too). Its witness: the ring above, `j` = 18, which meets the
sharper law's premises and not the present law's (18 is inside c2's
words). Unmutated, word 18 reads 9 after the join; mutated, 0: checked
by `bend --check-only` on the witness both ways. The present law is
true of the mutated code, yet `scripts/one_law.py` counts the mutation
caught against it too, because the mutation breaks its proof (in
`unlink_closed.go`). A mutation counted caught shows the proof broke;
only a witness meeting the law's premises shows the law false of the
mutated code. The witness runs only where the checker crashes, so this
distinction is not made by the gate: a lesson for `docs/Proving.md`.

Verdict: adjust before the merge, to the sharper claim, with the
mutation and its witness.

## F2. Premises on the word, or a list of the words written

The first choice the agent named. The laws take the word `j` with a
premise for each region it must avoid: off the ring, not the head, not
either faced port's link word (form A). The alternative is a function
computing the words a join writes, `join.writes(h, x, y)`, and one
premise that `j` is not among them (form B).

Form B is exact, but its list is computed from the heap as the join
finds it: the two ports read and the two predecessors the walks find.
A serve makes several joins, inserts and allocations in turn; a serve
law in form B would name each step's list, so each step's heap, in its
statement: the intermediate heaps a frame law is meant to hide.

Form A names regions, which a serve law can name once: the ring's
links, the nodes the serve frees and makes, the head, and the link
words of the ports its crossings faced, which the serve knows from its
template, not from intermediate heaps. Each join's premises then follow
from the serve's, region by region; an insert adds a member, and
`off.links` over the ring with it implies it over the ring without.
The path law (`.host.path`) will speak in the same terms: a node on a
walk's path is no member of the ring and no node the serve made.

Verdict: keep form A, with F1's regions.

## F3. Cursors stay listed: where that stops serving

The model's `closed` asks every listed member's word 3 to hold a listed
member, not that the ring be one cycle. After a join, x and y are freed
but stay listed, and the ring is still closed, since a free leaves word
3 alone. The agent chose this as enough for the write set, and the laws
so far hold with it. It stops serving as soon as freed nodes are reused,
and a serve reuses them next.

In the example the free list after the join runs c3 (20), then c1 (12),
then the old head: last freed, first taken. The next serve's splice
takes fresh nodes through `P.node` (`glam/splice.bend:38`), which writes
a non-data node's header and its payload word, word 3
(`node.fin`, `spike/kprim.bend:547-549`). So the first node spliced
lands at 20 and writes word 23. The ring listed as s, c1, c2, c3 is then
no longer closed: c3's word 3 holds a payload. And a crossing made a
cursor at a placeholder taken from 12 is inserted only if it is apart
from the ring (`insert_closed`'s `apart`), which fails while c1 is
listed.

A serve after a join needs the ring without x and y: closed for s, c2
needs that no member still names c1 or c3 in its word 3, that each had
one predecessor. That is the ring a cycle. The plan's cycle lemma
(`.host.ring`) gives it from the walk; the same can be had by the
model's shape: the ring as its members listed in walk order, each
member's word 3 the next listed, the last the first, the members
distinct. Unlink is then the list without x, insert the list with x
after situ, the join's ring the list without x and y, and the node a
walk finds before x is x's predecessor in the list, which closes
`unlink_keeps`'s missing piece exactly. It follows the repository's
advice to prefer state shapes in which an invariant holds by
computation (`AGENTS.md`, Laws).

Verdict: open question for the maintainer. The agent recommends the
exact ring as the next rung, before `.host.serve`.

## F4. The premises taken, not derived

- **The faced ports' link words are no member's word 3** (`off.links`).
  With the maintainer's answer: a port's link word is its node's base
  plus one plus the port's number, so `u + 1` is member m's word 3 only
  where `u` is m's port 2. A cursor's only port is port 0 and the
  situating node has none, so it holds where every link word holds a
  static or a port its node has, and every member is a cursor or the
  situating node. Neither is a law yet: the first is a well-formedness
  invariant of the host's net, the second the ring's own. An aside: the
  rule table gives the cursor kind arity 1 for every form
  (`spike/krules.bend:51-52`), the situating node's form included; a
  well-formedness law should say that node's word 1 is never linked.
- **x and y node bases** (`P.base(x) == x`). Every member came into the
  ring as a node `P.node` made, at `(fh - 1) << 2` (`node_index`,
  `spike/LAWS.bend`); clearing the low two bits of a word shifted left
  by two leaves it as it was, which should follow from `lawful-words`'
  bit laws (no package states it, `ArrayLaws_2026-11-13.md` F5). A
  small rung, once the ring is exact (F3): its members are then the
  nodes inserted.
- **A cursor inserted is apart from the ring** (`insert_closed`). It
  follows from the placeholder being taken from the free list, where no
  node in use lies (the free list's exactness, stated today over a
  model, not over `P.alloc`), and from freed members being out of the
  listed ring (F3).

Verdict: keep as premises; derive the node bases after F3, the ports'
link words with the net's well-formedness, freshness last.

## Question 3, sketched: exact write sets

At the maintainer's request, a small try of the exact form, in a scratch
worktree, nothing of it on `main` but this section. The aim: to see the
tradeoffs, not to adopt it.

What was built, all proved:

- `RG.ws.join(h, x, y)`, the words `join(h, x, y)` writes, computed by
  the join's own reads: each faced port's link word unless static, the
  word 3 of the predecessor each walk finds, on the heap after the link
  and on the heap after the first unlink, the head, and x's and y's four
  words. The heaps between the steps stay inside the function; and
  `RG.off(ws, d, j)`, the word `j` none of the list's, with its lemmas
  for appended lists.
- `join_keeps_ws`: every word off `ws.join(h, x, y)` reads as before.
  Its premises: the heap's words perfect, and `off`. Nothing of the
  ring, of node bases or of the ports: every word the join writes is in
  the list by construction, wherever the walks stop.
- `join_ws_bridge`: `join_keeps`'s premises on the ring and on `j` give
  `off(ws.join(...), d, j)`. The ring's reasoning lands here: the ring
  closed through the link and the first unlink, each predecessor a
  member, so its word 3 off `j`. `join_keeps` is then the exact law
  composed with the bridge.

What each cost, in `glam/PROOF.bend`'s text (one law's recheck alone
33 s either way, the imports' cost, so time does not separate them):

| | lines | characters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `join_keeps`'s composition | 47 | 32,800 |
| its step lemmas (link, unlink, free, the ring kept closed) | 199 | 21,100 |
| `join_keeps_ws`'s composition | 53 | 103,100 |
| `ws_eq`, the list computed on the heaps between | 18 | 26,700 |
| the exact form's new step lemmas and `off`'s | 111 | 10,700 |
| `join_ws_bridge` | 35 | 33,600 |

The exact law's text is three times the region law's though its steps
are as many: each entry is taken from a list written out in full, the
heaps between steps in it. The bridge costs what the region law's
composition cost, since it is that reasoning moved.

Who pays for the ring's reasoning:

- In the exact form, the frame law costs nothing to apply, and steps
  compose by appending their lists: two joins in turn keep every word
  off `ws.join(h0, x1, y1) ++ ws.join(h1, x2, y2)`, `h1` the heap the
  first left, with no premise on the ring at all. But no consumer wants
  "off a computed list" for itself: `.host.path` wants a node of the
  walk's path left alone, and to show its words off the list it crosses
  the bridge at each step, on that step's heap, with the ring's facts
  there.
- In the region form, each law crosses the bridge once, inside its
  proof, and a consumer composes premises. For the same two joins: `j`
  no member's word 3, off the four joined cursors, the head and the
  four faced ports' link words, every one read on `h0`, the second
  join's ports carried back to `h0` by the first join's law, which the
  sharpening of F1 now allows.
- Either way, the second join needs the ring as the first left it: the
  exact ring's work (F3), which the exact form does not spare.

What each catches. A witness on a ring of seven, joining its first and
third cursors, with x unlinked twice and y never (the second
reviewer's F9): the second walk never meets x, runs out its million
steps of fuel and stops before a fifth cursor, whose word 3 it writes.
That word is off the exact list, which the checker confirmed, and a
member's word 3, which the region form allows. Run, the mutated join
changes it (32 to 16), the unmutated leaves it: so the exact law is
false of this fault, and the region law true of it. The checker could
not run the mutated walk itself, a million steps, within fifteen
minutes: this one was shown by running the code, not by a proof. The
second reviewer's other survivors, x freed twice and a port linked to
itself, write only words both forms allow: they leave out y's free and
the other port's link, and only the positive laws see what is left out.

And the reverse. `ws.join` reads with the code's own walk, so a fault in
that walk is in the list too: the mutation `walk_by_position` (the walk
following word 2) leaves the exact law true, the list moving with the
writes, while the region law, through `pred_in_ring`, is false of it.
`scripts/one_law.py` counts this mutation caught against both, only
because a lemma both proofs share (`go_step`) breaks: the lesson of F1
again. This is `docs/Proving.md`'s "A specification sharing the
decisions": an exact set computed by the code's own reads claims
nothing of those reads.

The agent's reading, for the maintainer's judgment:

- Exact sets fit steps whose writes are fixed by what the serve already
  names: the splice's fresh nodes and their links, from the template,
  where no walk decides and the list is plain data, needing no code's
  reads to compute.
- Regions fit steps where a walk decides what is written (an unlink, a
  find): there an exact list shares the walk's decisions, and the
  claims that matter, that the walk stopped where it should, belong to
  the ring's laws (the exact ring, F3), which catch the double unlink
  too.
- A serve law could take both: the splice's writes as a list, the
  joins' as regions, the serve's statement in regions, which is what
  `.host.path` will read.
