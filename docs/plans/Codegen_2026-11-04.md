# Generation and specialization

Drafted for the maintainer's review, before the kernel grows further (the
maintainer, 2026-11-04: work out an idea or plan for generation and
specialization before pushing forward much further). The spike's kernel
has struggled with sharing: a helper or a second caller on a hot path
cost every rewrite 5% to 10% more than once, and threading one more
template argument through the chain took a mechanical edit of thirty
signatures and four hundred generated rewrites. This plan gathers what
we know of how Bend compiles our code, audits the kernel as it stands,
and sets out the choices: what must be specialized and what may share,
by which vehicle, for which strategies. Where a step states an option it
is a starting point, not a choice.

## What we know

Measured this far (`docs/BendIssues.md`, HeapModel's measurements, the
agents' notes):

- Every Bend function becomes a C function: a leaf (`spin_N`, `static
  inline`, unnamed in the C) or a segment of the work loop
  (`WL_FID_<module>_<function>_<instance>`, never inlined, its
  continuations `_k<n>`). A call returns its results through memory, with
  a status check.
- Bend folds a chain of functions into one segment only while each has
  one caller. A second caller, or a branch whose arms both call the
  continuation, makes a function a segment of its own: `go.ext.r` with
  three callers cost about 100 instructions a rewrite, 9% on demand
  (HeapModel's `.links.dead-ports`, gcbench run 223).
- Leaves are clang's to inline, by its heuristics: a leaf called from
  many sites stays out of line (`P.fuse` at four sites in demand's
  rewrite segment), and a small change elsewhere can flip a decision and
  move a build by up to 15% (`spike/tools/inline_build.sh` forces inlining
  to see the work itself). Small helpers on the hot path cost about 56
  instructions a rewrite even so (`.links.dead-ports`).
- Arithmetic and comparisons compile inline; `&&` and `||` over them too.
  A `match` on a computed value is refused; the helper it takes is a call.
- Records and tuples flatten into a function's arguments and results:
  about 30 instructions an interaction for each word of a strategy's
  state.
- A template (`~` parameters) is compiled once for each family it is
  given: the kernel's chain once per strategy (plain and stack demand,
  eager, two stacks, profiling, each with and without events). The kernel
  with demand's families alone takes Bend's compiler 1.9 GB; with every
  family, the library's program, 3 to 4.5 GB.
- Generated code is written by Bend programs and checked current
  (`generated-sources`): today one generator, `spike/tools/gen_krules.bend`,
  writes the rewrites, the dispatch tables and the kind tables from the
  rule table, as Bend source built of strings.

## The audit

`spike/tools/sharing.py audit OUT [PROGRAM]` (built 2026-11-04, below)
takes a program's C, clang's record of the calls it left out of line
(`-Rpass-missed=inline`), and names Bend's unnamed leaves by matching
each to the Bend definitions with its parameters, narrowed by what each
calls (of runmode's 1,964 leaves, 521 named alone and 208 among two or
three; the rest mostly Base's, or with parameters the C does not keep).
Its baseline on main at d13902b, for runmode (demand plain and
collecting):

- Demand's rewrite segment (`WL_FID_knet_go_2`) leaves out of line:
  `P.fuse` at four sites, `P.alloc` at one, `P.dup`, `P.peel_ctl`,
  `KR.check` (805 lines), `P.val_is_fn.tab`, and six generated rewrites
  of 50 to 73 lines (unnamed: hundreds share their parameters). The
  interpreted table's calls sit in its continuations, cold.
- The strategies: demand's walk `wk.push` and the family's interaction
  entry; the collection `sd.mw`, `P.popq`, the events' drain;
  collecting demand's `dc.chunk.go`.
- About 700 functions in spike/ have two call sites or more, kprim's
  primitives first (`P.fld` 146, `P.form` 134, `P.rd` 106, `P.renode` 76,
  `P.link_x` 55). Most are leaves clang inlines; the cost is where it
  does not, and in segments a second caller made.

## Steps

1. `codegen-review` — the maintainer reviews this sketch; what is decided
   goes to `docs/decisions/`, the rest to the steps below.
2. `codegen-audit` — built 2026-11-04: the audit above, a command of
   `spike/tools/sharing.py`, beside `compare`, which diffs two commits'
   segments with their call sites (HeapModel's
   `heap-strategies.generate.sharing`). Run before and after a change to
   a hot path; its numbers are a baseline, not a test.
3. `codegen-policy` — tentative — what must be specialized and what may
   share. A starting point: each strategy's hot chain, from taking a pair
   to its rewrite's last write, is one segment, every function on it with
   one caller and every leaf inlined (the audit shows none left out of
   line); work that is rare is done in a branch that exists anyway (a
   kind pair's own rewrite), never tested for on the shared path; cold
   code (the interpreted table, audits, loaders, reports) shares freely,
   compiled once.
4. `codegen-hand` — tentative — fix the audit's hot findings by hand
   first, to learn the stakes before building a tool: `P.fuse` and
   `P.alloc` made to inline in demand's rewrite segment (a copy each per
   call shape, or a smaller body), measured on the four builds.
5. `codegen-vehicle` — tentative — how variants are made. Options:
   - Templates and discipline, the audit as the check (now). Cheapest;
     each change to a hot path needs care, and a hook every family
     threads touches every signature.
   - The generator grown into the kernel's chains: gen_krules.bend's way,
     Bend source built of strings. One language, already working; code in
     strings is hard to read, and its errors appear only in the output.
   - A preprocessor of our own, in Bend (`generated-sources` allows one),
     over sources that stay valid Bend, its directives in comments. A
     first directive, a copy per caller: each call of a marked function
     gets its own copy, so that none has two callers and a chain folds
     into one segment; then a family directive, a chain written once and
     emitted per family with its hooks substituted, so that a family that
     runs no events has no `after` to call, and static conditionals for
     what one family does and another does not. Needs a reader for
     enough of Bend: definitions, parameters, calls.
6. `codegen-families` — tentative — which programs compile which families:
   the library's program builds every strategy (3 to 4.5 GB, about 55 s),
   where a runtime would build two or three; whether the rarely measured
   families (two stacks, profiling) share one generic chain at a cost in
   their own speed, to halve the build.
7. `codegen-names` — tentative — leaves the audit cannot name: generated
   rewrites share their parameters, hundreds alike. Naming them through
   Bend's JS output, which names every function (`$knet_go`), by matching
   the two call graphs; or a parameter a rewrite carries for its name.
8. `codegen-upstream` — tentative — what Bend could give, for the
   maintainer to raise if they choose: names for leaves in the C, an
   inline or outline hint, specialization by call site. Tracked in
   docs/BendIssues.md once raised.

## Open questions

- The vehicle (`codegen-vehicle`): a preprocessor over valid Bend, the
  string generator grown, or templates with the audit alone.
- Directive syntax, if a preprocessor: comments in valid Bend, so that
  the hand-written source still checks, or a template language of its
  own.
- Scope: the spike's kernel alone, or glam's runtime later too.
- Which strategy families are worth a specialized chain, and what build
  memory and time are acceptable.
