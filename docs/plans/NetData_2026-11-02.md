# Data in nets: values, references and heaps

Drafted for the maintainer's review; a net's capacity is decided, the rest
is to come, after HeapModel's `heap-strategies.erasure.load` steps (the
maintainer, 2026-11-03). The spike has
treated data as a placeholder while it worked out the nets themselves: a
`U32` number, a builtin, a lazy handle, unit. The maintainer (2026-11-02):
this area needs a lot more attention, a proper sketch of how we work with
shared and borrowed heaps, remote lazies and collection, and perhaps
structured data within a net, to pipeline lists or compute local
fixpoints over dictionaries. This plan gathers the questions, what is
decided already, and the facts they rest on. The answers are to come with
the maintainer; where a step states an option it is a starting point, not
a choice.

## What is decided

- glam's values (`docs/DistilledDesign.md`): numbers are exact rationals,
  unbounded; lists are ropes, texts and binaries among them; dicts; functions;
  raw nets.
- `split-heap`: data holding no lazy or function handle is plain Bend data,
  shared and freed by Bend; whatever holds lazy state lives in the store.
- `store-pages`: a handle is one `U32`, page and slot; cells never move.
- `store-collection`: the store has its own collector.
- `net-nodes-double`: a net holds its nodes in one flat array of its own,
  doubling from 128 nodes; a value a node cannot hold in a word goes in the
  net's own data table, indexed from a spare word; an entry may reference
  lazies through handles, but never owns a cell.
- `header-kind-byte`: a node's header is a 6-bit kind, 2 bits of form, a
  24-bit field.
- `net-capacity`: a net holds at most 2^28 nodes, so a reference stays
  below 2^30.
- `operator-nodes`, `functions-are-nets-with-arity`,
  `copy-reduced-function-nets`: builtins become operators; a function is a
  net and its arity, its copies sharing its reductions.
- `packed-texts-and-binaries`: texts and binaries are lists, stored four
  bytes a word in rope leaves.
- `intern-hot-values`: Bend counts a shared node's owners in 24 bits;
  widely shared values are interned, handles passed instead.
- The heap model's sketch ([`HeapModel_2026-10-27.md`](HeapModel_2026-10-27.md),
  The model): final and partial data, cells, forcing, and values crossing
  into a net, numbers inline and other values as table entries.

## What the spike has

- A data node (kind 5) holds its value in its header's form and field and
  one word: form 1 a `U32` number in the word; forms 2 and 3 a builtin, its
  op and remaining arity in the field, nothing or one number collected in
  the word; form 0 an entry of the net's data table (an `Array<Val>`), its
  index in the word. An entry is owned by its node alone: a copy rule
  duplicates it, an erasure drops it, the sweep drops it with the node.
- Placeholder values (`spike/val.bend`): `VUnit`, `VNum` (a `U32`),
  `VFun` (a builtin and its arguments), `VLazy` (a handle), and two forms
  internal to the kernel.
- Every leaf costs a node of four words: a literal, an eraser, a void.
- The kernel's ceilings follow `net-capacity`: 2^30 words of nodes, 2^28
  words of a strategy's pairs.

## Steps

1. `netdata-review` — the maintainer reviews this sketch; what is decided
   goes to `docs/decisions/`, the rest to the steps below.
2. `netdata-capacity` — decided 2026-11-02, `net-capacity`: at most 2^28
   nodes a net. The kernel had stopped at 2^21 nodes and 2^22 words of
   pairs, the sizes of the first flat kernel's preallocated arrays
   (`spike-node-layout`, 4c15d44), kept as the doubling array's ceiling
   with no record deciding them. The agent's earlier statements that
   references stay below 2^23 or 2^24 rested on that ceiling, and the 2^24
   conflated it with the header's 24-bit field and with Bend's reference
   counts, which bound sharing, not sizes. The options weighed were Bend's
   own bound, 2^29 nodes, an array at depth 31, with no high bit to spare;
   2^28, the reference's top two bits free, as the collection's walk
   already assumed for its tags; 2^24, six bits free; and the old 2^21,
   which `succ` at k 21 passed, ending with no value and 15 errors. The
   ceilings are now 2^30 words of nodes and 2^28 words of pairs, and a law
   (`capacity_leaves_tags`) keeps references below the walk's tags. On
   demand, `succ` at k 21 and k 22 now end done, in 389 and 753 ms, and at
   k 26 (2^27 nodes) in 11.6 s, 4.1 GB at the peak of the last doubling
   (the default build at the step's commit). The bound itself, 2^28
   nodes, needs about 8 GB at that peak, more than this machine has free,
   so the out-of-memory path at the bound is the one tested at the old
   ceiling. The data table needs no bound of its own: it reuses freed
   entries, each owned by one node.
3. `netdata-refs` — the reference word. Today a reference is a node's word
   and a port (0 to 2) in its two low bits; port value 3 names no port,
   only `nil`. A reference whose port bits are 3 could be static: no node,
   the reference standing for a leaf (one port, its principal), in the one
   link word of the port it faces, nothing pointing back. The layout to
   start from, the maintainer's direction (2026-11-02): a few low bits of
   type, the largest feasible small integers, packed pointers with the
   28 bits a node's index has (`net-capacity`), small rationals, perhaps
   small binaries, no kind or form encoded at the cost of a whole
   subspace, and whatever else there is squeezed under one suffix, its
   class in the next two bits:

       bits 1..0  00 01 10   a node's port: node index in bits 31..2
                  11         static:
       bit 2      0            integer, 29 bits two's complement (31..3)
       bits 3..2  01           packed pointer, 28 bits (31..4)
       bits 5..2  0011         small rational, 26 bits (31..6)
                  1011         reserved, 26 bits (31..6)
                  0111         small binary or text, 26 bits: a length
                               in two bits, up to three bytes
                  1111         the rest, its class in bits 7..6, 24 bits
                               each (31..8):
       bits 7..6    00           builtin: its op and the arguments it
                                 still needs, as a header's field holds
                                 them (`operator-nodes`)
                    01 10        reserved
                    11           constant: the eraser, all ones,
                                 nil's value too; the dead operator,
                                 unit

   Each constant is one value. The eraser is one, whatever its polarity,
   the one its port lacks, and shares nil's value, which no state stores
   (the maintainer's aside, 2026-11-03; HeapModel's
   `heap-strategies.erasure.load.cycle.walk.directed.dead-kinds.links.dead-ports`).
   A static's value means only what this layout gives it, and a static
   counts as the node it stands for (`static-links-count-as-nodes`).
   `1011` is reserved for now (the maintainer,
   2026-11-03), rather than merged with `0011` to give small rationals
   `011` in bits 4..2 and 27 bits, a sign among them: `netdata-numbers`
   may want it for small rationals, the pointers' kinds below for a class
   of their own; no static is stored beyond a run, so merging later
   changes constants only. A static never enters the collection's notes,
   which hold node references alone; its top bits are its own. To settle:
   what a packed pointer names (an entry of the net's table it owns; a
   value borrowed or shared, `netdata-borrow`; a remote lazy,
   `netdata-lazies`), which of those need a bit of their own, how a small
   rational splits its bits, and how texts and binaries packed in
   references relate to rope leaves.
4. `netdata-numbers` — exact rationals: small integers and small
   rationals immediate, larger ones as Bend data (which representation of
   big integers and of rationals, normalized how), arithmetic's fast paths
   and when they promote; the spike's `U32` placeholder retired, its
   builtins (`succ`, `add`, `fma`) restated over rationals.
5. `netdata-table` — the net's own table: what an entry holds (final data,
   handles), its ownership (one reference owns it; a copy rule shares Bend
   data by its reference count rather than copying deep), when it is
   dropped, how it grows, what happens to it when the net freezes.
6. `netdata-borrow` — values a net reads but does not own: a frozen
   function's constants, shared by every copy rather than duplicated into
   each; interned hot values; data of the heap the net was made in. A
   borrowed reference must not outlive its owner, and copying a net shares
   what it borrows. Whether a borrowed heap is a region the net names by
   index, or Bend data the table holds counted, is the question.
7. `netdata-lazies` — remote lazies: a reference to another cell's value.
   Demand needs it (state 2) and the scheduler resolves it; final data
   then enters shared, partial data stays a handle; forwarding chains; no
   cycle kept alive through tasks (the Rust bootstrap's
   lazy-owns-partial-progress). How a need is recorded so that resuming
   costs no walk.
8. `netdata-collection` — data's part in collection: entries dropped with
   their references (the directed sweep drops them now with their nodes),
   handles in a net's table marked by the store's collector, final data
   never walked, interned values' lifetimes, a table compacted when its net
   freezes.
9. `netdata-structures` — structured data inside a net: lists as nodes, a
   cons and an end, so a producer and a consumer stream through the net,
   pipelining; dicts as nodes, so a fixpoint over a dict (`self`) is
   computed within the net; against values opaque in the table. Their
   rules (matching as interaction), laziness element by element, and how
   they freeze into ropes and dicts (`arch-structures-spike` in
   [`Architecture_2026-10-26.md`](Architecture_2026-10-26.md)).
10. `netdata-measure` — workloads for each: arithmetic over large and small
    numbers, list streams, dicts with self-reference, texts.
